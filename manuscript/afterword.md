# Afterword — What Would Make This a Theory?

*A principle earns its reach by distinguishing what could happen next.*

Suppose two research programs inherit comparable resources. One repeatedly produces results that become usable in new instruments and productive processes. The other produces publications, disagreement, and no comparable return. Both consume energy. Both are far from thermodynamic equilibrium. A theory that explains their different trajectories must say more than either of those facts.

This is the task left by the book's creation cycle. Ontological uncertainty is its foundation; a gradient and constrained pathways jointly enable the next organization that opens energy access. Continuation is characterized by frequency and must be explained through autopoiesis. Further creation follows the three dialectical principles. These are the author's commitments, not consequences obtained by calling an object an assembly. A predictive theory must identify the mechanisms and distinguish its requirements from the outcomes it predicts.

The extended claims describe AI and data centers as products of natural processes and the universe and fundamental constants as probably assembled. The AI claim needs mechanisms connecting resources, constraints, learning, and deployment, with counterfactual comparisons of specific dependencies. The cosmological claim needs dynamics that explain fundamental conditions instead of simply inserting them. Neither follows merely from classifying its subject as an assembly.

The proposed ontological foundation adds a philosophical task: justify constitutive indeterminacy and the openness of categorical oppositions. Further creation then requires three explicit tests. For interpenetration, vary each proposed dependency and examine its effect on the other. For negation of the negation, identify which limiting relationship is overcome and which earlier capabilities the successor preserves and reorganizes. For quantity into quality, predefine the changing quantity, predicted threshold, and new regime. Missing dependence, simple replacement without the claimed reconstruction, or no predicted regime change counts against the particular dialectical account. Deterministic models can also exhibit these relations, so success would not uniquely establish the ontology. The applications are the book's proposed tests using the historical vocabulary in [S130](../research/sources.md#s130).

The book adopts natural experiments and explicit causal inference as its standard for demonstrating empirical causal arguments. Historical records can provide evidence of what a change caused without requiring us to recreate an industrial revolution, a research community, or a social order. The task is to identify a credible comparison with what would have happened without that change. New controlled experiments remain useful where feasible; their absence does not disqualify historical evidence.

The 2021 economics prize recognized David Card's empirical work and Joshua Angrist and Guido Imbens's methodological contributions to causal analysis. Natural experiments exploit variation arising outside a researcher's controlled intervention, including changes generated by institutions and policies. The event's being historical does not make it a natural experiment. Its assignment process must justify the comparison being made. [S103](../research/sources.md#s103)

A causal model states which variables influence which others and what assumptions permit an effect to be identified. Pearl's framework makes these commitments explicit; a diagram records assumptions rather than certifying that its arrows are true. Identification asks whether the desired effect follows from those assumptions and the available observations. Estimation then concerns its magnitude and uncertainty. [S104](../research/sources.md#s104)

For each argument, specify the unit of comparison, the change being investigated, the outcome, and the interval. A mine, a workshop, a person, and a research program are different units. The effect of cheaper delivered coal on engine adoption is different from its effect on net extraction, and both differ from the effect of additional resources committed to trials while fuel prices remain unchanged. Defining the question prevents one successful comparison from silently answering several others.

Begin by defining the precursor and the next organizational level through their components and dependencies. State which new relation is expected to enable energy extraction. That is the qualifying capability in the revised definition; its inclusion cannot serve as evidence that the hypothesis is true. Compare net returns, recurrence, and renewed production over a preselected interval, recording construction, operation, renewal costs, and external subsidies. A capable prototype that cannot regenerate its productive conditions is a failed continuation, not evidence for an achieved full cycle.

Then identify why some units encountered the change and others did not. If mines expecting rapid expansion obtained better fuel access, comparing them with slower-growing mines may confuse the effect of fuel with the prospects that prompted its provision. Historical correspondence, engineering records, administrative rules, and accounts of failed proposals become evidence about this assignment process. They help establish whether a comparison is defensible before a statistical method is chosen.

The crucial comparison has three stages. First vary the gradient and the proposed constraint separately and together, testing their joint contribution to formation. Then measure whether the new organization enables the specified energy access. Finally interrupt the claimed regenerative return while keeping external supplies and other relevant conditions comparable. Does the frequency of the organization fall, and can restoring the return recover it? Measure repair, replacement, offspring, and repeated operations separately. The aim is to identify the autopoietic cause of recurrence rather than infer it from abundance.

Specify what counts as the same organization and the denominator of its frequency: occurrences per opportunity, production per interval, or prevalence within a defined population. Measure losses and observation effort as well as appearances. Repeated production by an externally maintained apparatus is a rival explanation to self-production at the chosen boundary. Predeclare any wider organization being tested. Sustained recurrence of a qualifying energy-accessing assembly without the proposed regenerative dependence would challenge the necessity claim; changing the boundary after every failure would protect the wording rather than test the theory. [S02](../research/sources.md#s02), [S33](../research/sources.md#s33)

Early abiogenesis adds a timing question with its own selection problem. Separate the onset of suitable persistent environments, the earliest surviving biosignature, a molecular-clock estimate for a common ancestor, and the unobserved origin event. The first two are not automatically the endpoints of an observed waiting time. Account for preservation and for the requirement that life began early enough for observers to exist. The chapter 3 chronology is evidence to model, not a newly completed natural experiment or a measurement of selection for greater energy extraction. [S131](../research/sources.md#s131)–[S135](../research/sources.md#s135)

The prompt-formation hypothesis also requires an independent meaning of ready. Specify the ingredients, concentrations, reaction dependencies, energy coupling, losses, and predicted timescale before observing the outcome. Compare systems with similar material and energy supplies but different proposed enabling relationships. Does completing the specified network shorten waiting times and increase the frequency of sustained organization? A narrow set of allowed configurations is not itself a rate prediction. Repeated failure or long delays under the stated conditions would count against this mechanism. Do not redefine every failed preparation as unready solely because formation did not occur. The Platonic constraints proposed in I72 must become measurable relations or model restrictions with consequences; their being initially unseen cannot exempt them from comparison.

The vent proposal adds a specific comparison. Build on the controlled chemistry in S144 by varying the relevant gradient and mineral arrangement while measuring reactant consumption, product formation, and losses. Then test whether adding a candidate regenerative network improves sustained access after its construction and maintenance costs. Include an abiotic comparison under the same boundary and supply. Predefine whether optimal means useful-energy yield, throughput, or net renewal; these outcomes need not agree. This is a proposed test of the book's extension of Smith and Morowitz, not a result already established by their hypothesis or by the formate experiment. [S140](../research/sources.md#s140), [S144](../research/sources.md#s144)

One possible design compares changes over time in affected and comparison groups. Difference-in-differences requires a defensible account of how their outcomes would have changed without treatment, often expressed as parallel untreated trends. Similar earlier trends are informative but cannot prove that counterfactual. Treatment timing and differing effects must be handled explicitly rather than hidden in one indiscriminate before-and-after regression. [S106](../research/sources.md#s106)

Another design studies an assignment threshold. If a rule changes eligibility at a cutoff while other relevant conditions vary smoothly there, observations just to either side may identify a local effect. Precise sorting around the cutoff or another simultaneous change can undermine that interpretation. The result concerns the comparison supported near the threshold, not automatically every institution in the economy. [S107](../research/sources.md#s107)

An instrumental-variable design uses a source of variation that changes exposure to a proposed cause. Its interpretation requires that the instrument have an effect on exposure, be appropriately independent of other causes of the outcome, and affect the outcome through the specified exposure rather than a separate route. Further assumptions determine which effect is recovered; the familiar local-average-effect interpretation requires that the instrument not move some units toward exposure and others away from it. Angrist, Imbens, and Rubin show why the resulting effect may concern the subgroup whose exposure changes with the instrument. A causal estimate is not automatically a universal law. [S105](../research/sources.md#s105)

These are routes to investigate, not labels to attach to any convenient episode. An archive may support a causal model through other justified comparisons, or only partial bounds on an effect. If the relevant causes cannot be separated, the claim remains an association or an unresolved hypothesis. A detailed narrative can establish timing and mechanism without identifying the magnitude of a counterfactual effect. Its evidentiary contribution should be stated on those terms.

Consider a proposed study of the first engines. Suppose records revealed a change in delivered fuel costs whose timing and allocation were not responses to expected engine adoption. Investigators could examine whether exposed mines subsequently adopted fuel-hungry engines more often than defensible comparison mines. They would need evidence about earlier trajectories, ore quality, flooding, labor costs, finance, engineering access, and other concurrent changes. The causal model must distinguish prior common causes from consequences of cheaper fuel; adjusting away the latter could remove the very pathway being investigated. No such qualifying event or completed estimate is claimed here. The example specifies what the archive would have to supply.

Even that design would answer only part of the chapter's question. Cheaper fuel could enable operation without explaining where the design came from. Adoption could rise without increasing the net energy obtained after construction and operation are counted. To test the proposed support for costly intermediates, examine committed resources, unsuccessful trials, the interval before useful operation, and the resulting capacity. Identify each link and investigate alternative routes by which the initiating change could have affected the outcome.

This separates a demonstrated local mechanism from the book's larger physical explanation. Evidence that energy access caused adoption would support one connection in the synthesis. Evidence that adoption increased extraction would support another. Neither alone establishes a maximum of entropy production. That stronger claim must predict the choice, rate, or development of pathways in a way that rival accounts do not already predict. If the physical quantities cannot be reconstructed well enough, the energetic claim remains unresolved even when an institutional or technological effect is well identified.

The research design should state its main comparison before selecting favorable results from the records. Historical research cannot erase the investigator's knowledge of famous outcomes. It can use contemporaneous descriptions of constraints, define case selection without requiring success, distinguish exploratory work from confirmation, and test implications in further places or periods. Failed attempts, missing records, and systems that persisted without the predicted transition belong in the sample where the question requires them.

For every estimate, investigate competing events, changes in who enters the records, and effects spilling into the proposed comparison group. Report uncertainty and sensitivity to plausible violations of the assumptions. Tests using earlier dates or outcomes the mechanism should not affect can expose a bad design; passing them does not prove every assumption. A small, imprecise estimate cannot establish the absence of a mechanism. A precise result incompatible with a specified prediction is much more informative. These are the book's requirements for assessing its proposed comparisons, not analyses already completed.

Begin by specifying the system. For a mine, the boundary might include pumping, fuel supply, extraction, and the construction and repair needed to sustain them. For a research program, it might include training, instruments, experiments, communication, and the productive uses into which results enter. These are choices made for an explanatory purpose. We should state what crosses the boundary and how the conclusion changes when the boundary moves.

Next identify the disequilibrium. A name such as industrial society is not a physical measurement. The relevant differences might involve chemical potentials, temperatures, or other specified driving conditions. Their relation to the investigated activity has to be traced. If the only connection is that the participants ate food, the account has established energetic support without yet explaining the direction of inquiry.

Then describe the accessible assemblies. This set cannot contain every configuration a theorist can imagine. It must reflect available materials, construction operations, representations, skills, and time. Nor can accessibility be defined by the fact that an assembly eventually appeared. A useful model identifies the conditions that make a transition reachable before observing its success.

Feedback must also be represented through time. An earlier productive capacity can support later learning, whose results affect still later production. This ordering distinguishes reciprocal dependence from an instantaneous circular explanation. It also makes it possible to ask which earlier conditions change both subsequent investment and outcomes, and whether the records can separate those effects.

There are three coupled problems. Which flows occur under the imposed constraints? How does an organization form and change the accessible pathways? Which recurring operations regenerate its organized conditions? These are the distinct tasks assigned to the energetic account, assembly, and autopoiesis. A model of development must explain their connections rather than infer one from evidence for another.

For the autopoietic claim, map the operations and what each renews. Identify the boundary, external inputs, replacement intervals, and reserves. Then specify an interruption and an available substitute independently of the outcome used to assess them. A missing wage payment might interrupt provisioning, while a reserve or different payment arrangement could preserve it. If the proposed dependency can be removed without the predicted effect and without a compensating operation, the regenerative account needs revision.

The historical language of guarantees can be tested in the same way. Identify a stated obligation, the capacity needed to honor it, the observed delivery, and the contribution expected in return. Investigate what happens when delivery fails: withdrawal, substitution, coercion, institutional repair, or breakdown. A promise and a fulfilled obligation are different evidence. The failure of one guarantee does not by itself explain the decline of an entire social order. The closure hypothesis requires evidence that the operations renewing a specified organization stopped, could not be adequately replaced, or came to reproduce different defining relationships. Continued outside exchange is compatible with regenerative closure; it is not evidence that closure has failed.

A simple computational investigation could represent assemblies as nodes and feasible transformations as directed connections. Each connection would have a construction requirement, a timescale, and specified effects on the subsequent physical flows. The model would represent the operations and resource requirements that retain or regenerate intermediate structures, allowing those connections to fail. This is a proposed research design, not a model already validated by the manuscript.

The closure test must be specified independently of whether the institution survived. Identify an observable failure of renewal, the reserves that could absorb it, and the available substitutes. Then predict whether the organization can continue on the same terms. Defining every survivor as closed and every failure as a loss of closure would make the account circular.

The mathematical frameworks used in the chapters make that proposed investigation more specific. Constraint satisfaction asks whether a bottleneck changes the dependencies among choices, as well as the number of choices. Stability analysis asks whether a disturbance decays or grows. Production-network analysis asks whether required components can be regenerated at feasible rates. Control theory asks which distinctions and responses permit the system to remain within viable conditions. Predictive complexity distinguishes retained structure from unpredictable variation. Each offers a different observable, rather than another name for complexity.

Use those distinctions to compare mechanisms. Hold the available resources fixed while changing the dependencies among attempts; ask whether search becomes more effective. Hold the production network fixed while interrupting a replacement flow; ask whether reserves and substitutes predict the timing of failure. Hold a controller's resources fixed while changing access to a relevant observation; ask whether its actions improve. These are proposed discriminating designs, not additional completed experiments. The existing chapter analyses retain their recorded limitations.

The synthesis requires a further comparison: do those mechanisms jointly explain the changes attributed to the energetic driver? Derive local transition rules first, then measure the resulting flows, retained capabilities, and losses. Compare with a model that has the same search and maintenance structure but lacks the proposed thermodynamic selection. A successful explanation must earn the coupling between these quantities; it cannot count every improvement in one as an increase in all.

The crucial choice would be how transition and renewal rates arise. If a simulation simply retains whichever future node extracts the most energy, it assumes the desired selection and gives the system foresight. Generate formation from local dynamics and available support, then calculate what each assembly can capture, what it loses, and what its actual operations can repair or reproduce. Ask whether greater extraction improves continuation and produces successively higher assemblies. Only after that should the model test whether the tendency approaches an entropy-production maximum. A promising future state must be able to remain inaccessible behind a barrier.

That design would distinguish lowering a barrier from increasing the resources directed toward it. It could also distinguish an assembly that immediately performs well from one that must be supported through an initially costly phase. The mine and the citrate experiment suggested this second pattern through very different mechanisms. A general model should preserve the difference while testing whether the common transition problem explains anything additional.

The comparison needs alternatives. A model with the same formation opportunities but no dependence of renewal or reproduction on energetic returns tests whether that proposed connection matters. A profitability model may explain an industrial case, while communication and mathematical inheritance may explain a cluster of discoveries. The creation-cycle account must predict differences or derive successful regularities beyond a relabeling of those mechanisms. Comparing the supported cycle with and without an additional maximum-entropy-production rule is a separate test of the stronger conjecture.

Cognitive mechanisms allow more direct interventions. Give an artificial controller access to a specified model of its own activity, then remove or perturb that access while holding the task and resources comparable. Compare an alternative controller without that representation. A benefit would support a functional role for the model; it would not establish subjective experience. For the relation between cybernetics and autopoiesis, test both dependencies: what the controller helps renew and what renews the controller. Include reserves and replacement intervals before predicting a failure.

For language models, hold a checkpoint and task set fixed while separately changing prompting, the inference budget, and access to external feedback. Compare those effects with a specified training intervention. Record correctness, resource costs, repeated errors, plateaus, and regressions, not only the strongest result. Distinguish changes in context, external memory, and model parameters. These comparisons can reveal how a capability improves and where continuation stops helping. A claim of unlimited cognitive growth cannot be inferred from a gain over one tested interval. Follow any gain into an actual physical consequence before using it as evidence for the proposed energetic driver.

The historical studies should follow the same discipline. Use evidence from before a breakthrough to identify a proposed bottleneck, rather than defining the bottleneck by the eventual solution. Then ask what changed the bottleneck. Was an apparatus made possible? Did fuel become accessible? Was a useful representation developed? Did an institution begin carrying the costs of an intermediate result? What happened in comparable settings where that change did not occur?

Historical disruptions or allocation rules could also help investigate inquiry, but a loss of funding or an interruption of communication is not automatically independent of the prospects of the work. A credible study must establish the source of exposure and address spillovers between researchers. A grant threshold might identify an effect on a particular set of applicants without identifying why relativity emerged. Comparable discoveries and delayed or failed lines of work would have to support any generalization.

Relativity adds the problem of convergent content. Different researchers can share tools without producing equivalent theories. Their work can overlap in equations while differing in interpretation. A study of convergence therefore needs a criterion of equivalence appropriate to the question. Counting famous names is not enough. The counterfactual concerns whether a specified result would probably have appeared within a specified interval if one route or person had been absent.

The natural emergence of AI and data centers becomes testable only through more specific claims. State the prerequisites and observation interval before assessing the outcome, and investigate how those prerequisites arise. Test whether changes in access to resources, knowledge, and infrastructure alter the development of defined capabilities. Compare alternative architectures, failures, and substitutions. Robust convergence across histories would be informative, but the claim does not require the same outcome in every admissible history.

Data centers require their own comparison. Define the relevant concentration of computing equipment and supporting operations before examining alternatives. Compare histories in which coordinated facilities form with histories relying on dispersed computing under comparable productive constraints. If robust artificial cognition can develop without the predicted organization, that result bears against the data-center component of the thesis even if the AI component survives. Excluding every such history after the fact would remove the claim's content.

Failure cases have special value. A resource can remain unused because a second input is absent. A technically sound discovery can remain unadopted because the institution able to apply it lacks resources. A larger energy supply can intensify an unsuccessful search. A system can lower its throughput and remain more successful on a defined outcome. Each case identifies a place where the proposed coupling may stop or require another explanation. The two imagined collieries in chapter 2 specify such a failure in advance. Both pumps must uncover a seam. Only a return that reaches the repair shop should increase the chance of resuming work after a broken rod. Count that outcome separately from the initial lift. West’s observed Cornish duty figures answer a different question about work per bushel. They cannot fill the missing repair ledger.

The theory must also specify its clock. An instantaneous maximum, a stationary rate, and an average over a developmental history are different claims. A reserve can reduce present activity while preserving a future capacity. That possibility cannot license shifting the evaluation interval whenever a result is inconvenient. The interval and the reason for it should be part of the prediction.

Dialectics supplies questions for this work. What does the present organization enable? Which of the same relationships now block a further transition? What can be retained if that relation changes? Where are resources accumulating that could carry the cost of transformation? Which apparent oppositions arise from an inherited representation, and which express a physical incompatibility that a new vocabulary cannot remove?

These questions can guide prediction without promising a universal calendar. A factory organized around one source of power may have a recognizable bottleneck in its layout. A research community may possess several successful results that no current representation reconciles. An AI development loop may be limited by physical tests rather than candidate generation. The predicted transition should identify what must change and what observable consequence would follow.

Chapter 6 makes the ontology in I68 the foundation of its fine-tuning and cosmological assembly argument. The connecting physical account requires explicit premises. Define ontological indeterminacy and justify the claim that absolute nothingness would be a wholly determinate absence incompatible with it. Explain how the proposed indeterminacy could give rise to actuality and physical structure rather than leaving their emergence unspecified. The impossibility of complete determination, the impossibility of nothingness, and continual becoming are distinct claims; their connecting argument must be supplied. If time is an outcome, specify the ordering of becoming without assuming an external chronology. A structured vacuum and a quantum uncertainty relation do not provide that argument. Philosophical consistency and justified premises are one task; a physical model with observable consequences distinguishing it from alternatives is another. The additional connections to disequilibrium and maximum entropy-production rates also require derivation.

The cosmological extension must meet the same demand for an identifiable cause while using evidence appropriate to physics. We do not observe a set of universes assigned different fundamental constants, and ordinary variation among galaxies is not such an experiment. A model must instead derive distinguishing observable consequences from specified dynamics and assumptions; where available observations cannot distinguish competing origins, the claim is not identified. Cosmological history is not made unscientific by the impossibility of rerunning it, but the words natural experiment cannot supply a missing comparison.

The retention proposal in I73 supplies a narrower test. Hold a field's potential and starting state fixed while varying its modeled environmental coupling; separately vary the expansion history. Track coherence, occupation of each minimum, and escape rates, rather than treating them as one outcome. Compare the result with intrinsic longevity from the same potential. A claim of environmental retention is weakened if the effect disappears in the relevant regime or follows entirely from the assumed barrier. Connecting such a calculation to our universe then requires independent constraints and a distinguishing observable. These are proposed comparisons, not completed natural experiments. [S136–S139](../research/sources.md#s136)

The cosmological extension faces a higher threshold. It needs actual dynamics for the parameter or regularity being explained. A distribution of outcomes must follow from the model, rather than from an arbitrary list of possible universes. A thermodynamic extremum must be defined for the relevant physical setting. The theory cannot obtain its initial low-entropy condition simply by assuming that development begins with the gradient required to produce it.

“Probably assembled” is the book's provisional judgment, not a probability already measured or computed. A model should distinguish the universe's assembly from the formation of objects inside it, and the origin of fundamental parameter values from changes in effective descriptions. Derive an observable relation or parameter constraint that alternatives do not predict, identify what would refute it, and state which rules remain assumed. If time itself is among the proposed outcomes, specify the ordering used by the construction. These requirements make the conjecture more definite without pretending its derivation has already been supplied.

Chapter 6 develops fine-tuning as a natural material process grounded in ontological uncertainty. Its connection of progressive constraints, stationary principles, symmetry breaking, recursive maintenance, and cumulative assembly must become specified dynamics. Define the starting conditions and target: an enduring universe, particular laws or constants, or some narrower physical relation. Derive the outcomes and their sensitivity to those conditions. A probability requires a justified measure; natural emergence alone supplies neither a frequency nor a uniquely required history. Failed trajectories must remain part of the model assessment.

The model must also explain how its constraints are produced and how its maintained structures persist. Check whether narrowing the apparent parameter space transfers the unexplained precision into a precursor, boundary condition, or choice of dynamics. Derive the relevant parameter relations and compare their observable consequences with alternatives. Showing that an improbability argument lacks a justified distribution can undermine that argument, but establishing a natural generative explanation requires a positive derivation. Existing late-time bounds such as the Oklo constraint address a different part of the problem and cannot supply that derivation.

Between its ontological foundation and its account of fine-tuning, chapter 6 investigates differentiation and the origin or persistence of cosmic disequilibrium. Define the relevant equilibrium comparison, scope, and physical mechanism, including how local equilibria fit the account. Then separately test the maximum-rate claim: define entropy production, the boundary, timescale, constraints, and accessible competing pathways. Neither an asymmetric state nor positive entropy production proves a maximum. The historical questions in I67 need their own derived outcomes, including the matter surplus, interaction asymmetries, dimensional organization, and temporal conditions. A common vocabulary cannot substitute for those derivations.

No isolated example answers all of these questions. The value of the connected inquiry is that it makes the unanswered links visible. It asks the same causal question across different domains while requiring each domain to supply its own mechanism. The generalization may succeed broadly, survive only in restricted forms, or give way to a family of related explanations. Those outcomes would teach different things.

The creation-cycle hypothesis can fail at identifiable links. A credible increase in usable-energy access may fail to improve renewal or reproduction after its costs and other inputs are accounted for. A later assembly may repeatedly persist with lower extraction under the same specified comparison. A proposed energetic gain may depend entirely on a continuing external subsidy that the model omitted. A supposed successor may have formed through an unrelated mechanism. These outcomes require revising the stated domain or rejecting the relevant claim; changing the boundary or timescale after seeing them would remove the test.

The book's ambitious claim is that creation proceeds through this complete relation: a gradient and constraints make the next energy-accessing level possible; autopoiesis accounts for its recurring organization; dialectical transformation produces further conditions. The qualifying access capability, its sustained net return, the necessity of regenerative recurrence, and the three principles of transformation must face distinct comparisons. Ontological uncertainty and a maximum entropy-production rate require additional arguments. The tests must allow the complete cycle to fail even when one of its components succeeds.

## Afterword notes

The proposed graph model, historical comparisons, and counterfactual tests are research designs developed for this book. They have not been implemented or empirically validated here. The distinction between a restricted entropy-production principle and the broader assembly hypothesis follows the discussion in chapter 2 and [S07](../research/sources.md#s07). Biological and social autopoiesis are distinguished in chapters 3 and 9 and [S33](../research/sources.md#s33), [S10](../research/sources.md#s10). The proposed historical tests of guarantees draw on the bounded examples in chapter 9 and [S81](../research/sources.md#s81), [S84](../research/sources.md#s84)–[S86](../research/sources.md#s86). Existing models of technological combination and lock-in are acknowledged in chapters 5 and 7 and [S60](../research/sources.md#s60); the proposed model must be compared with them rather than claimed as the first account of retained combinations. Sources for relativity and its distinct historical problems appear in chapter 4 and [S65](../research/sources.md#s65)–[S67](../research/sources.md#s67).

The cognitive interventions extend chapters 10 and 11. The attention-model experiment is bounded by [S94](../research/sources.md#s94); prompting, reasoning-oriented training, test-time computation, agent loops, and intrinsic self-correction are distinguished in [S95](../research/sources.md#s95)–[S102](../research/sources.md#s102). The comparisons proposed here have not been carried out for this manuscript.

The methodological standard draws on the Nobel committee's [scientific background](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2021/10/advanced-economicsciencesprize2021.pdf), [Pearl's overview](https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r350.pdf), and the instrumental-variable, difference-in-differences, and regression-discontinuity sources [S103](../research/sources.md#s103)–[S107](../research/sources.md#s107). These references establish methods and their assumptions; they do not validate this manuscript's historical or thermodynamic claims.

The mathematical support is documented in [S120](../research/sources.md#s120)–[S127](../research/sources.md#s127), with control theory under [S45](../research/sources.md#s45) and technological combination under [S60](../research/sources.md#s60). The narrative applications are distinguished from the published models; the added conceptual comparisons do not change the numerical research results.
