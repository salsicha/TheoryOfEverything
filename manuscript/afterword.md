# Afterword — What Would Make This a Theory?

*A principle earns its reach by distinguishing what could happen next.*

In October 1834 the witnesses at Fowey Consols measured what William West’s engine could do with a bushel of coal. Fourteen years later the directors of the South Devon Railway gave up a service that had actually carried passengers by atmospheric pressure. In both places a pressure difference did work. In Cornwall it expanded what a mine could extract. In Devon the cost of keeping the valve tight counted against the next train. A theory of creation ought to tell us where to look, before the outcome, for the difference between those histories. [Cornish trial and railway records](05-how-disequilibrium-builds-a-new-pathway.md#chapter-notes)

The primary event to explain is the arrival of a capacity a system did not previously possess. Which combination of a usable gradient and inherited constraints made a particular new organization reachable? What could it now do, and which successor did that bring within reach? There is a further question about frequency. Once the new assembly appears, some of what it gains can enter the work of producing it again. Autopoiesis explains recurrence at that stage. Explaining why an arrangement endures still leaves the question of how it first became possible.

The extended claims describe AI and data centers as products of natural processes and the universe and fundamental constants as probably assembled. The AI claim needs mechanisms connecting resources, constraints, learning, and deployment, with counterfactual comparisons of specific dependencies. The cosmological claim needs dynamics that explain fundamental conditions instead of simply inserting them. Neither follows merely from classifying its subject as an assembly.

The proposed ontological foundation adds a philosophical task: justify constitutive indeterminacy and the openness of categorical oppositions. Further creation then requires three explicit tests. For interpenetration, vary each proposed dependency and examine its effect on the other. For negation of the negation, identify which limiting relationship is overcome and which earlier capabilities the successor preserves and reorganizes. For quantity into quality, predefine the changing quantity, predicted threshold, and new regime. Missing dependence, simple replacement without the claimed reconstruction, or no predicted regime change counts against the particular dialectical account. Deterministic models can also exhibit these relations, so success would not uniquely establish the ontology. The applications are the book's proposed tests using the historical vocabulary in [S130](../research/sources.md#s130).

## What changes the next starting point?

Saltom’s pumping and winding installations stood on either side of the same shaft. More winding power alone could do little for a seam miners could not enter. Lowering the water made horses at the surface productive in a different way, and getting coal away from the beach made Ravenhill’s winding shaft valuable in turn. These changes give us three objects of inquiry, the first accessible seam, its repeated working and a new use of the neighboring shaft. One could measure a different success in each place. [Saltom and Ravenhill](01-the-square-with-one-answer.md)

The crucial comparison has three stages. First vary the gradient and the proposed constraint separately and together, testing their joint contribution to formation. Then measure whether the new organization enables the specified energy access. Finally interrupt the claimed regenerative return while keeping external supplies and other relevant conditions comparable. Does the frequency of the organization fall, and can restoring the return recover it? Measure repair, replacement, offspring, and repeated operations separately. The aim is to identify the autopoietic cause of recurrence rather than infer it from abundance.

Specify what counts as the same organization and the denominator of its frequency: occurrences per opportunity, production per interval, or prevalence within a defined population. Measure losses and observation effort as well as appearances. Repeated production by an externally maintained apparatus is a rival explanation to self-production at the chosen boundary. Predeclare any wider organization being tested. Sustained recurrence of a qualifying energy-accessing assembly without the proposed regenerative dependence would challenge the necessity claim; changing the boundary after every failure would protect the wording rather than test the theory. [S02](../research/sources.md#s02), [S33](../research/sources.md#s33)

Early abiogenesis adds a timing question with its own selection problem. Separate the onset of suitable persistent environments, the earliest surviving biosignature, a molecular-clock estimate for a common ancestor, and the unobserved origin event. The first two are not automatically the endpoints of an observed waiting time. Account for preservation and for the requirement that life began early enough for observers to exist. Those dates constrain a history. Measuring which pathways gained greater access to energy would take independent evidence. [S131](../research/sources.md#s131)–[S135](../research/sources.md#s135)

The prompt-formation hypothesis also requires an independent meaning of ready. Specify the ingredients, concentrations, reaction dependencies, energy coupling, losses, and predicted timescale before observing the outcome. Compare systems with similar material and energy supplies but different proposed enabling relationships. Does completing the specified network shorten waiting times and increase the frequency of sustained organization? A narrow set of allowed configurations is not itself a rate prediction. Repeated failure or long delays under the stated conditions would count against this mechanism. Do not redefine every failed preparation as unready solely because formation did not occur. The Platonic constraints proposed in I72 must become measurable relations or model restrictions with consequences; their being initially unseen cannot exempt them from comparison.

The vent proposal adds a specific comparison. A physical model should track how the mineral arrangement and the chemical gradient affect consumption, product formation and losses. Then test whether adding a candidate regenerative network improves sustained access after its construction and maintenance costs. Include an abiotic comparison under the same boundary and supply. Predefine whether optimal means useful-energy yield, throughput, or net renewal; these outcomes need not agree. This is a proposed test of the book's extension of Smith and Morowitz, not a result already established by their hypothesis or by the formate experiment. [S140](../research/sources.md#s140), [S144](../research/sources.md#s144)

The unit of comparison changes the question. At Saltom the fuel under the boiler paid for lifting the water. Coal from the workings could feed fires far beyond the pit. At Fowey Consols the mine imported coal to reach metal ores. A study confined to the engine house could compare pumping duty in the two places and miss what made either pump worth building. The boundary and the interval have to be stated before the result is known. [The fuel accounts](05-how-disequilibrium-builds-a-new-pathway.md)

Next identify the disequilibrium. A name such as industrial society is not a physical measurement. The relevant differences might involve chemical potentials, temperatures, or other specified driving conditions. Their relation to the investigated activity has to be traced. If the only connection is that the participants ate food, the account has established energetic support without yet explaining the direction of inquiry.

Which configurations were actually accessible? Tang Tingshu’s estimate at Kaiping supplies an answer with a price attached. A proposed larger colliery implied a traffic in carts that the district could scarcely furnish. His railway estimate changed which levels of mine output it made sense to pursue. A theory should be able to use that kind of evidence before learning whether the railway was built. [Tang’s transport comparison](05-how-disequilibrium-builds-a-new-pathway.md#three-hundred-carts)

Feedback must also be represented through time. An earlier productive capacity can support later learning, whose results affect still later production. This ordering distinguishes reciprocal dependence from an instantaneous circular explanation. It also makes it possible to ask which earlier conditions change both subsequent investment and outcomes, and whether the records can separate those effects.

There are three coupled problems. Which flows occur under the imposed constraints? How does an organization form and change the accessible pathways? Which recurring operations regenerate its organized conditions? These are the distinct tasks assigned to the energetic account, assembly, and autopoiesis. A model of development must explain their connections rather than infer one from evidence for another.

The test of recurrence needs its own evidence. The forty francs owed to Mlle Pommet can be traced to a hearing and an acknowledged debt. The surviving newspaper gives no date of payment. To learn how wages kept a workshop in operation would require records on both sides of that exchange, including what the household drew on during a delay. A justified claim can fail to put food on a table. [Pommet’s hearing](09-the-system-that-produces-its-tomorrow.md#chapter-notes)

In 1453 Alice Chaucer divided the Walsham demesne among twenty-two tenants at fixed rents. The land continued to yield crops under a changed relation between cultivation and income. To attribute that change to a breakdown in feudal guarantees requires evidence of which former service had failed, what else the estate tried and what fixed rents allowed both parties to do. The continued existence of an estate cannot decide what form of production existed on it. [Walsham and the terms of protection](09-the-system-that-produces-its-tomorrow.md#the-price-of-protection)

## A model that can fail

A simple computational investigation could represent assemblies as nodes and feasible transformations as directed connections. Each connection would have a construction requirement, a timescale, and specified effects on the subsequent physical flows. The model would represent the operations and resource requirements that retain or regenerate intermediate structures, allowing those connections to fail. This is a proposed research design, not a model already validated by the manuscript.

The mathematical frameworks used in the chapters make that proposed investigation more specific. Constraint satisfaction asks whether a bottleneck changes the dependencies among choices, as well as the number of choices. Stability analysis asks whether a disturbance decays or grows. Production-network analysis asks whether required components can be regenerated at feasible rates. Control theory asks which distinctions and responses permit the system to remain within viable conditions. Predictive complexity distinguishes retained structure from unpredictable variation. Each offers a different observable, rather than another name for complexity.

Three comparisons would help. Hold resources constant while changing the relations among available moves. Does a previously inaccessible class of results become reachable? Hold a production network fixed and interrupt a particular replacement flow. Do known reserves predict how long it can last? Give a controller new information about its own activity. Can it now do something that remained out of reach with the same time and equipment? Each question asks for a different observation.

The synthesis requires a further comparison: do those mechanisms jointly explain the changes attributed to the energetic driver? Derive local transition rules first, then measure the resulting flows, retained capabilities, and losses. Compare with a model that has the same search and maintenance structure but lacks the proposed thermodynamic selection. A successful explanation must earn the coupling between these quantities; it cannot count every improvement in one as an increase in all.

The crucial choice would be how transition and renewal rates arise. If a simulation simply retains whichever future node extracts the most energy, it assumes the desired selection and gives the system foresight. Generate formation from local dynamics and available support, then calculate what each assembly can capture, what it loses, and what its actual operations can repair or reproduce. Ask whether greater extraction improves continuation and produces successively higher assemblies. Only after that should the model test whether the tendency approaches an entropy-production maximum. A promising future state must be able to remain inaccessible behind a barrier.

That design would distinguish lowering a barrier from increasing the resources directed toward it. It could also distinguish an assembly that immediately performs well from one that must be supported through an initially costly phase. The mine and the citrate experiment suggested this second pattern through very different mechanisms. A general model should preserve the difference while testing whether the common transition problem explains anything additional.

The comparison needs alternatives. A model with the same formation opportunities but no dependence of renewal or reproduction on energetic returns tests whether that proposed connection matters. A profitability model may explain an industrial case, while communication and mathematical inheritance may explain a cluster of discoveries. The creation-cycle account must predict differences or derive successful regularities beyond a relabeling of those mechanisms. Comparing the supported cycle with and without an additional maximum-entropy-production rule is a separate test of the stronger conjecture.

Cognitive mechanisms allow more direct interventions. Give an artificial controller access to a specified model of its own activity, then remove or perturb that access while holding the task and resources comparable. Compare an alternative controller without that representation. A benefit would support a functional role for the model; it would not establish subjective experience. For the relation between cybernetics and autopoiesis, test both dependencies: what the controller helps renew and what renews the controller. Include reserves and replacement intervals before predicting a failure.

For language models, hold a checkpoint and task set fixed while separately changing prompting, the inference budget, and access to external feedback. Compare those effects with a specified training intervention. Record correctness, resource costs, repeated errors, plateaus, and regressions, not only the strongest result. Distinguish changes in context, external memory, and model parameters. These comparisons can reveal how a capability improves and where continuation stops helping. A claim of unlimited cognitive growth cannot be inferred from a gain over one tested interval. Follow any gain into an actual physical consequence before using it as evidence for the proposed energetic driver.

In the case of relativity, specify which achievement was within reach. Two papers can agree on a transformation and differ on what clocks measure. Convergence on the equation and convergence on the interpretation are different claims. A theory that explains the first has to do more work to explain the second.

The natural emergence of AI and data centers becomes testable only through more specific claims. State the prerequisites and observation interval before assessing the outcome, and investigate how those prerequisites arise. Test whether changes in access to resources, knowledge, and infrastructure alter the development of defined capabilities. Compare alternative architectures, failures, and substitutions. Robust convergence across histories would be informative, but the claim does not require the same outcome in every admissible history.

Data centers require their own comparison. Define the relevant concentration of computing equipment and supporting operations before examining alternatives. Compare histories in which coordinated facilities form with histories relying on dispersed computing under comparable productive constraints. If robust artificial cognition can develop without the predicted organization, that result bears against the data-center component of the thesis even if the AI component survives. Excluding every such history after the fact would remove the claim's content.

At Saltom an expensive attempt at a harbour drew capital and lost to the storms. At Kaiping the flooding was real even when Tang’s proposed output had no affordable route to market by cart. In Cheng’s chip comparisons, a learning method sometimes lost to an older search procedure on routed wire length. These cases matter because the gradient or demand for a solution can be quite genuine while a particular means of reaching it fails. A predictive account has to admit both outcomes. [The mine](01-the-square-with-one-answer.md) · [The chips](12-the-machines-that-build-the-next-machines.md)

The theory must also specify its clock. An instantaneous maximum, a stationary rate, and an average over a developmental history are different claims. A reserve can reduce present activity while preserving a future capacity. That possibility cannot license shifting the evaluation interval whenever a result is inconvenient. The interval and the reason for it should be part of the prediction.

Dialectics supplies questions for this work. What does the present organization enable? Which of the same relationships now block a further transition? What can be retained if that relation changes? Where are resources accumulating that could carry the cost of transformation? Which apparent oppositions arise from an inherited representation, and which express a physical incompatibility that a new vocabulary cannot remove?

At Kaiping, transport costs gave a reason to expect that a change on the road would alter decisions made underground. In 1905, the demand for consistent changes of frame reduced the range of equations a physicist could use. In the chip studies, a bound on how late a signal could arrive changed which layout counted as a success. Each yields a different prediction about what an extra unit of work could accomplish and where to expend it. [Tang](05-how-disequilibrium-builds-a-new-pathway.md#three-hundred-carts) · [Poincaré](04-the-discovery-that-became-possible.md#a-result-that-could-travel-by-post) · [Chip placement](12-the-machines-that-build-the-next-machines.md#somewhere-to-put-a-memory)

## Where the laws come from

Chapter 6 makes ontological uncertainty the foundation of its fine-tuning and cosmological assembly argument. The connecting physical account requires explicit premises. Define ontological indeterminacy and justify the claim that absolute nothingness would be a wholly determinate absence incompatible with it. Explain how the proposed indeterminacy could give rise to actuality and physical structure rather than leaving their emergence unspecified. The impossibility of complete determination, the impossibility of nothingness, and continual becoming are distinct claims; their connecting argument must be supplied. If time is an outcome, specify the ordering of becoming without assuming an external chronology. A structured vacuum and a quantum uncertainty relation do not provide that argument. Philosophical consistency and justified premises are one task; a physical model with observable consequences distinguishing it from alternatives is another. The additional connections to disequilibrium and maximum entropy-production rates also require derivation.

For cosmology the question is what trace an earlier history would leave in the universe we can observe. Two proposed origins might yield the same measured constant today. Could one also predict a relation between two other quantities, or a pattern at some scale, that its rival would have difficulty producing? Such a prediction would give a history of assembled laws a consequence beyond the values it was devised to explain.

The vacuum-retention proposal supplies a narrower test. Hold a field's potential and starting state fixed while varying its modeled environmental coupling; separately vary the expansion history. Track coherence, occupation of each minimum, and escape rates, rather than treating them as one outcome. Compare the result with intrinsic longevity from the same potential. A claim of environmental retention is weakened if the effect disappears in the relevant regime or follows entirely from the assumed barrier. Connecting such a calculation to our universe then requires independent constraints and a distinguishing observable. [S136–S139](../research/sources.md#s136)

The cosmological extension faces a higher threshold. It needs actual dynamics for the parameter or regularity being explained. A distribution of outcomes must follow from the model, rather than from an arbitrary list of possible universes. A thermodynamic extremum must be defined for the relevant physical setting. The theory cannot obtain its initial low-entropy condition simply by assuming that development begins with the gradient required to produce it.

“Probably assembled” is the book's provisional judgment, not a probability already measured or computed. A model should distinguish the universe's assembly from the formation of objects inside it, and the origin of fundamental parameter values from changes in effective descriptions. Derive an observable relation or parameter constraint that alternatives do not predict, identify what would refute it, and state which rules remain assumed. If time itself is among the proposed outcomes, specify the ordering used by the construction. These requirements make the conjecture more definite without pretending its derivation has already been supplied.

The fine-tuning hypothesis in chapter 6 makes two claims we can pull apart. Start with one dimensionless ratio, so a change of units cannot change the answer. Let the ratio emerge from a model and trace how work drawn from a specified gradient restores the arrangement that fixes it. Interrupt that return of work during the period of proposed renewal. If the arrangement recovers just as well, passive stability may account for the result. Then compare reachable arrangements under the same supply of energy and over the same interval. Calculate the work each can sustain per unit time before looking at which one persists. If a lower-power arrangement consistently prevails over an accessible rival that can sustain a higher rate throughout that interval, the maximum-extraction claim fails for this case. The ratio could still have been assembled.

The model must also explain how its constraints are produced and how its maintained structures persist. Check whether narrowing the apparent parameter space transfers the unexplained precision into a precursor, boundary condition, or choice of dynamics. Derive the relevant parameter relations and compare their observable consequences with alternatives. Showing that an improbability argument lacks a justified distribution can undermine that argument, but establishing a natural generative explanation requires a positive derivation. Stability over a later interval can constrain a proposed history. The question of how the value arose still stands.

Between its ontological foundation and its account of fine-tuning, chapter 6 investigates differentiation and the origin or persistence of cosmic disequilibrium. Define the relevant equilibrium comparison, scope, and physical mechanism, including how local equilibria fit the account. Then separately test the maximum-rate claim: define entropy production, the boundary, timescale, constraints, and accessible competing pathways. Neither an asymmetric state nor positive entropy production proves a maximum. The historical questions about asymmetry need their own derived outcomes, including the matter surplus, interaction asymmetries, dimensional organization, and temporal conditions. A common vocabulary cannot substitute for those derivations.

No isolated example answers all of these questions. The value of the connected inquiry is that it makes the unanswered links visible. It asks the same causal question across different domains while requiring each domain to supply its own mechanism. The generalization may succeed broadly, survive only in restricted forms, or give way to a family of related explanations. Those outcomes would teach different things.

The creation-cycle hypothesis can fail at identifiable links. A credible increase in usable-energy access may fail to improve renewal or reproduction after its costs and other inputs are accounted for. A later assembly may repeatedly persist with lower extraction under the same specified comparison. A proposed energetic gain may depend entirely on a continuing external subsidy that the model omitted. A supposed successor may have formed through an unrelated mechanism. These outcomes require revising the stated domain or rejecting the relevant claim; changing the boundary or timescale after seeing them would remove the test.

The book’s ambitious claim is that creation changes what the world can do next. A gradient and constraints make a new energy-accessing level possible. Autopoiesis helps explain how often it occurs once formed. Its dialectical transformation then changes the possibilities its successors inherit. The qualifying access capability, its sustained net return, the necessity of regenerative recurrence, and the three principles of transformation must face distinct comparisons. Ontological uncertainty and a maximum entropy-production rate require additional arguments. The tests must allow the complete cycle to fail even when one of its components succeeds.
