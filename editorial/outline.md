# Modern Historical Materialism

Physical and Platonic constraints narrow what can form. An energy gradient supplies work, and a structure that survives changes the conditions for the next structure. The book argues that this succession takes the quickest feasible route to the highest rate of entropy production the whole system can sustain. Its physical, biological and historical examples test different steps in that claim. [Expanded ideas](ideas.md) · [Reading order](../README.md#contents)

### 1. From Nothing to Asymmetry

The chapter argues that indeterminacy rules out absolute nothingness and traces what a physical history must still explain, from the common constants a region could inherit to the handedness of weak decay, the excess of matter over antimatter and the arrow of time. ### 2. From energy to structure

Heat flowing through water sustains convection rolls, while cooling water can leave a stable ice crystal, showing how constraints in Platonic space shape both dissipative and conservative self-assembly. [Read chapter 2](../manuscript/book/02-from-energy-to-structure.md)

<a id="chapter-03"></a>

### 3. From hydrogen to gold

Gravity gathers cooling gas into stars, and fusion and neutron capture supply later generations of stars and planets with elements ranging from carbon to gold. [Read chapter 3](../manuscript/book/03-from-hydrogen-to-gold.md)

<a id="chapter-04"></a>

### 4. From matter to life

At deep-sea vents, the proposed hierarchical entropy rejection funnel retains the products of successive reactions until a compartment can build its own wall and pass that capacity to a daughter. [Read chapter 4](../manuscript/book/04-from-matter-to-life.md)

<a id="chapter-05"></a>

### 5. Self Reproduction

Autopoiesis begins when a cell makes the boundary that sustains its own chemistry, and division lets a daughter inherit the means to do the same. [Read chapter 5](../manuscript/book/05-self-reproduction.md)

<a id="chapter-06"></a>

### 6. Self Assembly

Dissipative adaptation, maximum power and constructal law ask which flows favor a structure, while downward causation, work-constraint cycles, assembly theory and the adjacent possible explain how it shapes what can form next. [Read chapter 6](../manuscript/book/06-self-assembly.md)

<a id="chapter-07"></a>

### 7. Self Domestication

The chapter examines whether communities that killed habitual aggressors over the past 200,000 years favored more pliant temperaments and whether changes in neural crest development helped narrow the human face. [Read chapter 7](../manuscript/book/07-self-domestication.md)

<a id="chapter-08"></a>

### 8. Self Modeling

A delayed touch and a decision to pay for a second look show how the brain models its own actions and uncertainty, while dissipative adaptation offers a physical explanation to test for how repeated activity alters the circuits that make those judgments. [Read chapter 8](../manuscript/book/08-self-modeling.md)

<a id="chapter-09"></a>

### 9. Feudalism and Capitalism

From a Suffolk manor to the Leeds cloth trade, control over the land, labor and fuel needed to do work explains how people reproduce an economic order and when they can change its terms. [Read chapter 9](../manuscript/book/09-feudalism-and-capitalism.md)

<a id="chapter-10"></a>

### 10. Chariots and Steam Engines

Spare wheels kept chariots running at Knossos, and at a flooded mine the work of burning some coal to reach more coal paid only where miners could get the extra fuel to buyers. [Read chapter 10](../manuscript/book/10-chariots-and-steam-engines.md)

<a id="chapter-11"></a>

### 11. Morality and language

A drawing of a slave ship and the petitions it inspired show how people transmit a moral judgment until strangers can act on it together. [Read chapter 11](../manuscript/book/11-morality-and-language.md)

<a id="chapter-12"></a>

### 12. Bubbles and Bursts

The boom left fiber available for new networks, and advertising auctions made access to a reader’s attention valuable enough to pay for serving the next search. [Read chapter 12](../manuscript/book/12-bubbles-and-bursts.md)

<a id="chapter-13"></a>

### 13. Fine tuning paradox

The chapter asks whether a common physical history fixed the values of the constants and whether a clock inside a symmetric universe can register time while the whole remains stationary. [Read chapter 13](../manuscript/book/13-fine-tuning-paradox.md)

<a id="chapter-14"></a>

### 14. Levinthal’s paradox

A protein reaches a working fold because favorable contacts narrow its paths, offering a physical mechanism for the claim that living chemistry can quickly find ways to tap an energy gradient. [Read chapter 14](../manuscript/book/14-levinthals-paradox.md)

<a id="chapter-15"></a>

### 15. Fermi paradox

Life could arise in a planet’s first habitable interval and still have sent no living visitors if suitable worlds had to wait for element-rich Population I stars and their inhabitants have little reason to migrate until those stars decline. [Read chapter 15](../manuscript/book/15-fermi-paradox.md)

<a id="chapter-16"></a>

### 16. Attractor Paradox

The chapter argues that relativity arrived at its first opportunity, with Poincaré presenting a rule for moving observers and Einstein submitting his own derivation twenty-five days later. [Read chapter 16](../manuscript/book/16-attractor-paradox.md)
