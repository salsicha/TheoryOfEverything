# The World That Builds Itself

*A Material History of Everything*

## The argument

A five in a Sudoku column settles four empty squares. At a hydrothermal vent, a mineral pore holds some molecules long enough to react and lets others wash out. The vent adds what a paper puzzle lacks. A chemical gradient supplies work, heat and spent material leave, and the products that remain change which reactions can occur in the next parcel of water. The book calls this succession a *hierarchical entropy rejection funnel*. Physical and Platonic constraints limit the steps open at each level.

The book argues that assembly takes the quickest feasible route to the greatest rate of entropy production the system can sustain. Two pumps offered for the same flooded seam give that claim a test. How soon could each draw water? How much coal would reach buyers and burn over its working life, after allowing for construction, fuel and freight? The pump that starts first may lose on the second count. ## Part I · Physics

<a id="chapter-01"></a>

### 1. From zero energy to asymmetry

The chapter argues that early fields assembled the constants of a symmetric, expanding universe and that every symmetric stage eventually gives rise to an asymmetry, as gravity turned tiny density differences into the energy gradients that fed the first stars. ### 2. From Hydrogen to Gold

In the entropy funnel, escaping light lets a gas cloud contract until fusion begins, and some of the elements stars shed later form minerals whose iron reacts with seawater to sustain a chemical energy gradient at Lost City. [Read chapter 2](../../manuscript/book/03-from-hydrogen-to-gold.md)

### 3. From Matter to Life

Where hydrogen-rich vent fluid meets seawater, the proposed entropy funnel runs from a pore that retains carbon compounds to a cycle that grows faster than the current washes it away, then to a daughter that inherits the chemistry to build its own wall. ## Part II · Humans

<a id="chapter-04"></a>

### 4. Self-Reproducing (Autopoiesis)

A model cell makes the wall that keeps its catalyst inside, and a dividing alga passes on the nitrogen-fixing partner each daughter needs to grow in nitrogen-poor water. [Read chapter 4](../../manuscript/book/05-self-reproduction.md)

<a id="chapter-05"></a>

### 5. Self-Modeling

Darwin noticed which facts slipped his memory and made himself write them down, putting hierarchical and recursive self-modeling to work each time he encountered evidence against his theory. [Read chapter 5](fourteen-chapter/self-modeling.md)

<a id="chapter-06"></a>

### 6. Self-Domesticating

Lee’s account of the killing of /Twi opens a proposed history in which communities that killed habitual aggressors favored a milder, more pliant temperament, and inherited changes in neural crest development could also have produced narrower faces. [Read chapter 6](../../manuscript/book/07-self-domestication.md)

## Part III · Technology

<a id="chapter-07"></a>

### 7. Feudalism and Capitalism

On a Suffolk manor the loss of farmhands changed what a lord could demand, and in the Leeds wool trade competition for cloth orders drove merchants to back machinery while dependence on wages gave workers reason to organize. [Read chapter 7](../../manuscript/book/09-feudalism-and-capitalism.md)

<a id="chapter-08"></a>

### 8. Chariots and Steam Engines

A spare wheel could put a chariot back in service at Knossos, a steam pump kept a coal seam dry at Saltom, and at Kaiping Tang needed three hundred carts a day to sell the coal a new pump could free. [Read chapter 8](../../manuscript/book/10-chariots-and-steam-engines.md)

<a id="chapter-09"></a>

### 9. Morality and Language

In 1792 the Commons agreed to a *gradual* end to the slave trade, and the *Brooks* kept sailing even as its image showed strangers what happened below deck. [Read chapter 9](../../manuscript/book/11-morality-and-language.md)

## Part IV · Paradox

<a id="chapter-10"></a>

### 10. The Fine-Tuning Paradox

In the proposed hierarchical entropy rejection funnel, an early field sheds energy in steps that link the weak scale to the vacuum energy, and the particles produced could help restore the state whose properties later observers measure as constants. [Read chapter 10](../../manuscript/book/13-fine-tuning-paradox.md)

<a id="chapter-11"></a>

### 11. The Immediate Evolution Paradox

A zircon that records early water and a model that places favorable RNA chemistry around 4.33 billion years ago put bounds on the claim that Earth took ages to cool and acquired life in its first suitable interval. [Read chapter 11](fourteen-chapter/immediate-evolution.md)

<a id="chapter-12"></a>

### 12. Levinthal’s Paradox

Anfinsen’s enzyme can reconnect four bonds in 105 ways, and what determines whether it cuts RNA again is how the chain folds as those bonds close. [Read chapter 12](../../manuscript/book/14-levinthals-paradox.md)

<a id="chapter-13"></a>

### 13. The Fermi Paradox

If life first arose on worlds around the younger, element-rich Population I stars and only a dying home star makes migration worthwhile, a galaxy already home to life and its probes could still be waiting for its first living interstellar travelers. [Read chapter 13](../../manuscript/book/15-fermi-paradox.md)

<a id="chapter-14"></a>

### 14. The Simultaneous Discovery Paradox

Once the clocks and equations left a single rule for converting measurements between moving observers, Poincaré presented it in Paris and Einstein submitted his derivation twenty-five days later. [Read chapter 14](../../manuscript/book/16-attractor-paradox.md)
