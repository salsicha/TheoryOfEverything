# Did mines choose profit or maximum entropy production?

**Of 27 investments for which the forecasts disagreed, operators reported declining 17 and implementing or firmly scheduling 10.** The five-year profitability rule predicted adoption in all 27. Maximizing operating entropy production at the existing level of service predicted retention in all 27. That gives the operating-rate rule 17 correct calls (63%) and profitability 10 (37%). Simply predicting that operators would keep their equipment also gets 17 right. The test finds no extra predictive power for entropy production over that baseline. At the sole coal assessment with complete inputs, profitability got two of three decisions right. [Frozen forecasts](../scripts/results/mine_investment_forecasts.csv) · [Decisions with inputs, estimates and responses](../scripts/results/mine_investment_decisions.csv) · [Numerical results](../scripts/results/mine_investments.json)

**For the book’s district-wide maximum, there were zero scoreable disagreements.** The audits estimate electricity saved at a given service. They do not say how much additional ore a cheaper process would make economic, where the freed power would go or how much energy each investment took to build. Boddington’s pre-construction plans show why this matters. The design the mine built used the least energy per tonne in the engineers’ comparison and, at its proposed throughput, would use the most motor energy per year.

When two proposed upgrades at the same site received different responses, the result went the other way. In an exploratory comparison of ten such pairs with opposing forecasts, operators favored the higher-NPV proposal seven times and the one with greater operating dissipation three times. [Pair comparisons](../scripts/results/mine_investment_pairs.csv)

## How the test worked

The US Department of Energy’s Industrial Training and Assessment Centers visit operating facilities, prepare engineering recommendations, then normally ask clients about implementation six to nine months later. We took the entire qualifying roster through fiscal 2024 from the 22 November 2026 workbook. It includes **45 mining, quarrying and mineral-preparation assessments and 291 offers**. We used the visit’s recommendation fields for resources saved and the capital and other cost components. We excluded the client-reported implementation cost and the follow-up status from the predictors. The workbook is a retrospective export. Its public history cannot establish that the original values were never revised. [Program protocol](https://itac.university/assessment-protocol) · [Database manual, appendices II–III](https://itac.university/file/technical/IAC_DatabaseManualv10.2.pdf) · [Assessment roster](mine-investments/assessments.csv) · [All proposal inputs](mine-investments/recommendations.csv)

We committed the roster, rules and predictions before opening the eligible operators’ answers. The primary rule required a proposal with a positive investment cost, both cost components explicitly present and a positive saving in electricity at the site. Demand charges could affect the bill. Only metered energy units entered the physical estimate. This admitted 28 proposals at 11 assessments. Another 76 had only one recorded cost component and enter a sensitivity check. The remaining 187 failed one or more input requirements. Keeping the incumbent was a feasible alternative at the time of the offer. Mines could adopt several recommendations. [Dated protocol and file fingerprints](mine-investments/protocol.md) · [Forecast commit](https://github.com/salsicha/TheoryOfEverything/commit/39ce56b)

For profitability, we subtracted the up-front investment from five years of estimated annual bill savings discounted at 10%. The horizon and rate are common assumptions. Earlier research on industrial audits found that many firms used a shorter payback hurdle, which motivated the two-year check. For operating entropy production, we compared equipment doing the same useful work, with unchanged material flows, no change in exported work and a common final sink at 293.15 K. Each kWh of electrical work that would otherwise be dissipated adds 3.6 MJ divided by that temperature to the entropy generated. A retrofit saving electricity lowers this rate under those conditions. Neither the booked savings nor actual entropy production were metered in the database. Each result row includes the extra cumulative energy that would have to be dissipated at the same sink to offset five years’ estimated savings. [Forecast calculations](../scripts/mine_investments.py) · [Anderson and Newell on the audit estimates and payback thresholds](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.reseneeco.2003.07.001) · [Thermodynamic scope](sources.md#s07)

The program’s **I** includes completed work and definite plans within its follow-up window. **N** means the operator reported no implementation. Pending and unavailable responses stay unresolved. All 27 primary disagreements had an I or N. The archive certifies no individual completion date. Between zero and ten of the ten positive responses could represent work already complete when the operator answered. Nor can we tell which of several installations came first or follow the sites that closed before an assessment. This is a test of investment forecasts against reported decisions. It supplies no random assignment of technology to mines. [Status definitions, appendix III](https://itac.university/file/technical/IAC_DatabaseManualv10.2.pdf) · [Unfiltered follow-up extract](mine-investments/responses.csv)

## What individual mines reported

The Wyoming sub-bituminous coal assessment in 2003 is particularly informative because the same operator gave three answers. All three proposals had positive estimated five-year NPV and would have reduced operating dissipation for the same service.

| Proposed change | Up-front cost | Annual bill saving | Annual electricity saving | Operator response |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Fit more efficient belts or drive components | $6,580 | $2,530 | 73,400 kWh | Implemented or firmly scheduled |
| Fit compressed-air dryers to eliminate blowdown | $4,000 | $1,160 | 40,800 kWh | Declined |
| Repair leaks in compressed-air or inert-gas lines | $110 | $330 | 11,800 kWh | Implemented or firmly scheduled |

Those are three offers at one assessment, CO0532. At Nevada gold assessment OR0423 in 2002, the operator declined both adjustable-speed drive proposals, including one estimated to cost $300,000 and save $518,000 and 8.104 million kWh a year. At a Nevada copper assessment in 2022, the operator reported adopting or committing to efficient lighting and declining occupancy sensors. Even a short estimated payback did not guarantee a sale. The extracts give the offers and decisions, and the published recommendation codes identify the equipment involved. [Decision ledger](../scripts/results/mine_investment_decisions.csv) · [Official recommendation codebook](https://iac.university/technicalDocs/ARC%20List%20-%20V19.1.pdf)

| Comparison | Disagreements with a known response | Profitability correct | Fixed-service operating maximum correct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Primary five-year forecast | 27 | 10 | 17 |
| Two-year simple payback hurdle | 14 | 6 | 8 |
| Include offers with one missing cost component counted as zero | 89 | 36 | 53 |

The extra five-year offer on which both rules agreed was declined as both predicted. Three more conflicts in the expanded sample had unavailable responses. Counting all 92 gives between 36 and 39 correct for profitability and between 53 and 56 for retention. In the primary test, giving each assessment one vote averaged over its offers puts profitability at 38.6%. Omitting one assessment at a time puts its proposal-weighted share between 30.4% and 43.5%. The two-year rule is more sensitive to weighting. Retention wins eight of fourteen proposals, but profitability reaches 55% when each of the ten contributing assessments gets equal weight. The roster contains volunteered audits of promising opportunities. It cannot supply an industry adoption rate or say why a particular owner declined. ## Which of two proposed investments did the operator take?

Keeping the incumbent is different from choosing *between two upgrades*. After opening the follow-ups, we made an exploratory comparison using only the already frozen five-year NPV and electricity forecasts. At the same assessment, rank each pair by NPV and by operating entropy at fixed service. The second rule prefers the upgrade that leaves more electricity being dissipated. The 28 eligible offers generated 28 within-assessment pairs. The forecasts ranked 25 of the pairs in opposite orders. Operators declined both proposals in 11 pairs and adopted or firmly scheduled both in four.

In the **10 remaining pairs**, six assessments reported one positive response and one decline. **Profitability ranked the accepted proposal higher in seven, and operating entropy ranked it higher in three.** In Wyoming the belts offered a larger financial gain than the air dryers. The dryers would have preserved more electricity consumption. The mine reported accepting the belts and declining the dryers. Three of profitability’s seven matches involve the same Louisiana proposal. These ten comparisons reuse offers, and several investments could coexist. They reveal which option received a positive response, without establishing that a mine was obliged to choose exactly one. This comparison was specified after the primary score was known and is reported separately. [Every oppositely ranked pair](../scripts/results/mine_investment_pairs.csv)

## Machinery decisions with dated designs

**Boddington, Western Australia.** The mine engineers’ 2001 paper records the designs considered before Newmont’s board approved development in February 2006. Its tables give projected cost, throughput and motor energy per tonne. Multiplying the last two quantities allows a comparison the eventual choice alone could never supply. [Parker, Rowe, Lane and Morrell, tables 2–3](https://www.smctesting.com/about/publications/the-decision-to-opt-for-high-pressure-grinding-rolls-for-the-boddington-expansion) · [Newmont’s filing recording the board decision](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1164727/000119312506216043/d10ka.htm)

| Early crushing and grinding design | Planned million tonnes/year | Operating cost, A$/tonne | Motor energy, kWh/tonne | Motor energy at planned throughput, GWh/year |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conventional semi-autogenous mill | 11.2 | 4.18 | 23.1 | 258.7 |
| Precrushing followed by semi-autogenous milling | 20.8 | 3.32 | 20.2–21.2 | 420.2–441.0 |
| High-pressure rolls followed by ball milling | 25.0 | 2.95 | 18.0 | 450.0 |

For a fixed quantity of ore, the conventional circuit had the highest projected motor energy and the rolls offered the lowest operating cost. If the rolls let the operator process **over 28.3% more ore in the same period**, their total motor energy would exceed that of the conventional circuit, holding each design’s kWh per tonne constant. The proposed increase from 11.2 to 25 million tonnes cleared that threshold. At the capacities the engineers proposed, the rolls had the lowest unit operating cost and the highest estimated annual motor energy. The gap between the two larger designs is small enough to be sensitive to operating hours. The operator’s October 2010 site report documents four high-pressure grinding rolls in the plant and records ore milled. It confirms the machinery family chosen. Equipment sizes and reserves evolved after the early study. The calculated annual energy figures are engineering scenarios, and the engineers had already named their preferred design when they published. This named case is separate from the frozen US scores. [Newmont’s operating-site report, monthly milling and equipment inventory](https://s24.q4cdn.com/382246808/files/doc_presentations/2010/Oct_1_2010_Boddington_Site_Tour_Final.pdf) · [Transcribed inputs](mine-investments/mill-designs.csv)

**Morrison, British Columbia.** A 2008 engineering study compared two actual proposals at the same 10.95 million tonnes a year. On its central figures, pressure rolls cost C$9.451 million more to install and saved about C$7.34 million a year in operation. Applying the same five-year discount rule gives the rolls a C$18.36 million advantage over the conventional mill. The conventional design drew more operating power, 22.24 MW against 17.91 MW. Here the two forecasts pick different machines even at the full planned tonnage. The authors gave the cost estimates an accuracy range of ±25%. The company carried the rolls into its 2009 feasibility design. British Columbia refused the environmental assessment certificate in 2022. Neither circuit went into service under that proposal. A comparison counting approved projects alone would have lost this outcome. [Wardrop’s May 2008 study, table 5.1](https://www.pacificbooker.com/pdf/080514_HPGR_Trade_Off_Study.pdf) · [2009 engineering filing, section 16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1319150/000121716009000115/f2009techrep.pdf) · [Province’s decision](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2022ENV0011-000166)

**Tropicana, Western Australia.** The joint venture’s 2009 environmental assessment specified pressure rolls to lower energy use in its planned gold plant. The operating team documents that the rolls and ball mill came into service in September 2013. It also reports a revealing third option from the earlier feasibility work. Rolls followed by a pebble mill had an even larger projected NPV, but the team judged the pebble mill riskier. The source we located for the comparative financial figures was published after commissioning. The dated 2009 record establishes the proposed design, and the later account verifies the installation. The later figures earn no points in the forecast score. [2009 environmental assessment, section 5.4](https://www.tropicanajv.com.au/irm/pdf/d104c749-35d2-42f7-95c9-1532781cedad/B9-HAC-Greenhouse-Emission-Assessment.pdf) · [Operating team’s account, feasibility and commissioning](https://minedocs.com/21/Tropicana-Milling-Circuit-09122017.pdf)

## What the district comparison would have to count

At a Nevada gold site the rejected drive’s estimated saving amounts to 40.52 million kWh over five years. Extra ore processing or another consumer using that much additional electricity at the same sink would erase the operating difference. Energy used in constructing the competing systems also enters that account. The archive supplies neither those flows nor the counterfactual output if the company had chosen the other drive. No such installation can be assigned a district-level win from its electricity bill.

The 2018 federal environmental review for the Rosebud coal mine in Montana reaches the buyer, the Colstrip power plant. Its no-action scenario left other permitted mine areas available and assumed the power plants would continue operating through 2038. Counting every ton in the proposed extension as additional coal burned in the district would contradict the comparison the agencies actually made. The record supplies no paired private investment returns and entropy budgets across alternative suppliers. [2018 final environmental review](https://www.osmre.gov/sites/default/files/inline-files/508-Western-Energy-Area-F-Final-EIS-11-27-2018.pdf) · [2019 decision, section 3.4.1](https://www.osmre.gov/sites/default/files/inline-files/NEPA_RosebudMine_ROD.pdf)

The strongest implication for the book is a prediction that can change sign. Where buyers will take only a fixed tonnage, an efficient mill can earn more while dissipating less. Where a hard ore body, a limited power supply and a market for additional production give the mill room to expand, the same efficiency gain can raise total dissipation. The size of the required expansion can be estimated before the choice. Boddington supplies such a threshold. The US decisions show why retaining high-consumption equipment, by itself, supplies little evidence about the cause of the decision.

## Reproduce the results

The tracked input files are sufficient to rerun without a network connection. From the repository root, use the Python environment described in the [research guide](../scripts/README.md#run).

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/mine_investments.py forecast
.venv/bin/python scripts/mine_investments.py score
```

To reconstruct the public extracts from the original zip, place the matching archive at `scripts/data/ITAC_Database.zip`, then run `extract-inputs` and `extract-outcomes` with the same script. The live download changes over time. The extractor checks the original SHA-256, and scoring checks the hash of the forecasts committed before the answers were opened. The named engineering calculations use the small cited design extract and are reported separately in the JSON.
