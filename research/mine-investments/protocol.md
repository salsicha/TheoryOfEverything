# Mine investment forecasts made from assessment records

**Decision recorded on 22 November 2026, before inspecting the eligible clients’ follow-up answers.** This is a retrospective test with a forecast frozen in Git before scoring. The source is the US Department of Energy’s Industrial Training and Assessment Centers (ITAC) public workbook of 22 November 2026. We inspected its schema, sector codes and recommendation inputs to design the test. A web search exposed a 2026 Texas record outside the cutoff. The historical propositions and some famous mine outcomes were already known. [Archive and download](https://itac.university/download) · [Program protocol](https://itac.university/assessment-protocol) · [Database manual, appendices II–III](https://itac.university/file/technical/IAC_DatabaseManualv10.2.pdf)

## Cohort and alternatives

Start with **every assessment through US fiscal year 2024** classified as mining and quarrying, NAICS 212. When NAICS is missing, use SIC major groups 10, 12 or 14. An explicit nonmining NAICS takes precedence. These are assessments of operating sites. Mine preparation, milling and quarrying can be included in the industry classification. Repeated assessments cannot be linked to a named establishment with certainty. Include sites regardless of whether anything was subsequently adopted. The program is voluntary and its directory omits mines that closed before an assessment.

Each offered investment competes with continuing to operate the existing equipment. Assess all reported recommendations in the cohort. For the primary comparison require an energy recommendation (ARC category 2), positive annual site electricity savings (EC), and no other recorded resource except electricity demand charges (ED) and fees (EF). EC is kWh. ED and EF can affect bills but add no kWh to the heat calculation. Exclude missing physical quantities, missing dollar estimates for a listed resource, and ambiguous units. Require a positive investment cost and both recommendation cost components, IC_CAPITAL and IC_OTHER. An explicitly reported zero is valid. Exclude the client-reported IMPCOST, implementation status, payback field and post-visit commentary from the forecast.

## Rules fixed before scoring

- **Profitability.** Estimate five-year net present value at a 10% annual discount rate. The outlay occurs at the start. Estimated annual bill savings recur at year end. Adopt at positive NPV and retain at negative NPV. An exact tie supplies no directional forecast. The price, five-year service life and rate are common assumptions. Additional operating costs, downtime, financial constraints and changing output can change the owner’s preference.
- **Operating entropy production.** Hold mineral throughput, useful service, material streams, export of work and the final heat sink fixed across alternatives. Assume the avoided electricity would otherwise be dissipated as heat at 293.15 K within the boundary. For annual electricity savings of E kWh, the retrofit changes the average operating entropy production by −1,000 × E / (8,760 × 293.15) W/K. The incumbent ranks higher if the reduction is positive. Report the energy that construction or additional activity would have to dissipate over five years to erase that difference. These are conditional forecasts. The directory supplies engineering estimates, not measurements of entropy or achieved savings.
- **District-wide maximum.** Require comparable pre-decision estimates or bounds for construction, changes in extraction, displacement and supply, and the relevant physical flows for both alternatives. If their plausible ranges overlap or cannot be bounded, record the district ranking as undetermined. A reduction at a motor cannot by itself determine what a district does with its freed capacity. The primary site ranking tests a restricted version of the argument.
- **Scoring.** Freeze the dated cohort and forecasts first. In a separate pass, compare them with the program’s client follow-up. Code I as implemented **or definitely scheduled**, N as reported no, and pending, unavailable or blank as unresolved. Score head-to-head accuracy only when the forecasts differ and the follow-up is directional. Report how all unresolved decisions could affect the result and weight each assessment equally as a second summary. Treat decisions about several investments at one site as related. A funding award or a firm plan alone does not establish physical completion. For an independently documented installation, report its original forecast, operating record and chronology separately.
- **Sensitivity.** Recalculate with a two-year simple payback hurdle, and with the five-year model allowing one unreported cost component to be zero. Report coal assessments separately, the subset where both industry systems agree, and the narrower groups for lighting and efficient motor/drive or compressed-air measures. Report whether agreement is robust to omission of one assessment. All these rules compare a proposal with its own incumbent. Several proposals can be adopted together. The archive cannot identify the order in which machines were installed.

The earlier 1877 Kaiping freight forecast is an illustration. It provides neither a numerical entropy budget for both paths nor a set of independent decisions and earns no score here. ## Frozen inputs

The workbook contains 45 qualifying assessments and 291 offers. Before attaching the answers, the strict cost rule yields 28 forecasts at 11 assessments, of which 27 disagree. The workbook is a later export of visit estimates and follow-up answers. Its public version history does not certify that each cost cell was immutable after the visit. The two extracts omit all follow-up fields and retain every qualifying assessment and offer.
