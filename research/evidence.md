# Evidence by chapter

Study numbers and chapter references here follow the [twelve-chapter manuscript](../editorial/archive/twelve-chapter-outline.md). The [current outline](../editorial/outline.md) reorganizes the cases by subject. For the assumptions behind the estimates, see the [causal-inference guide](causal-inference.md).

The November 15, 2026 research update adds two analyses from primary public data: northern California kelp canopy and US fatal crashes around daylight-saving transitions. The current run contains four primary comparisons/reanalyses, one outcome-only analysis, two secondary diagnostics, one combined published-evidence record, and four cases without their required datasets. The research record for chapter 6 distinguishes its unidentified ontological foundation, the published granular mechanism, and the conditional Oklo bound. These are component studies; they do not demonstrate the book's universal hypothesis.

A separate chapter 2 study follows US coal mines through the 2019 tax cut and reported interruptions, and wind plants through the tenth anniversary of first production. The mine continuation gap widened by 3.11 percentage points, with missing records allowing a change between −0.76 and +3.88. All 75 underground incident-quarters had positive output the following quarter. No surface incident-quarter supplied a comparison. At wind plants the estimated change at ten years depended on the comparison window and calendar controls. The link from retained proceeds to a dated repair is still open. [Two-mine study and results](two-mines.md)

An investment test for the current chapter 10 examined 291 offers at 45 operating US mining and quarrying assessments. Of 27 conflicts with complete inputs, the profitability forecast matched 10 responses and the local operating-entropy forecast matched 17. Always predicting retention also matched 17. An exploratory comparison of upgrades at the same assessment favored profitability in seven of ten pairs with different outcomes. The Boddington and Morrison designs show how throughput and permission to build affect the comparison. [Mine investment study](mine-investments.md)

The [Elwha River study](elwha.md) tests a prediction shared by chapters 2 and 3. Removing two dams let Chinook carry marine material into upstream habitat again. The public surveys record that return, and separate brood-year data let us ask whether the fish replaced themselves.

Chapter 10 uses 555,134 national crash records from 2000–2015, with fixed geographic exclusions and explicit accounting for 64 retained crashes whose dates are incomplete. Assigning those crashes to all compatible dates bounds the mean event contrast between +0.0240 and +0.0510; these are measurement bounds, **not confidence intervals**. The analysis does not separate daylight, sleep, travel, or weather effects. Chapter 3 uses the original study's public supplementary workbook; its regional series supplies an observed outcome, not an untreated control group.
