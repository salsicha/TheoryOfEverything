# Working source ledger

Sources checked during preparation and drafting of the complete first manuscript on November 14, 2026. This is a research foundation for first drafts, not a completed bibliography. Search excerpts and abstracts support only the narrow claims identified below; relevant full-text passages were also consulted for chapter 2, including variational principles, the vacuum, effective theories, initial conditions, and the relaxion mechanism. Comprehensive literature review and scene-level verification remain necessary where specified. Source keys are used in `ideas`, `outline`, and chapter notes; the prologue and all twelve chapters have their own numbered notes. Abstracts and indexed passages are identified as limited evidence; access restrictions are not treated as full-text review.

## S01

**Constraint satisfaction.** Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, [constraint-satisfaction chapter](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/4th-ed/pdfs/newchap05.pdf); UC Berkeley CS188, [variable ordering](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/textbook/csp/ordering.html).

Supports the minimum-remaining-values heuristic and early detection of impossible branches. It does not establish a universal algorithm used by nature. The opening Sudoku experience comes from the author's notes; no benchmark results or reconstructed dialogue are claimed.

## S02

**Assembly theory.** Abhishek Sharma et al., [“Assembly theory explains and quantifies selection and evolution”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06600-9), *Nature* 622 (2023), 321–328.

Starting point for assembly index, copy number, and the authors' account of selection. The index depends on construction rules and reusable components; it is not a direct transcript of actual history. Claims of universal applicability and biosignature specificity require independent scrutiny. Chapter 3 now presents a bounded account alongside the independent criticism in S32; broader validation and the controversy remain open.

## S03

**Metabolic innovation and historical contingency.** Zachary D. Blount, Christina Z. Borland, and Richard E. Lenski, [“Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of Escherichia coli”](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0803151105), *PNAS* (2008).

Supports the example of aerobic citrate use in the long-term evolution experiment. Related mechanistic research: Erik M. Quandt et al., [“Recursive genomewide recombination and sequencing reveals a key refinement step…”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3926077/), *PNAS* (2014). Distinguish the initial innovation, its historical preconditions, and later refinement.

## S04

**Steam and economic constraints.** Robert C. Allen, [“Why the industrial revolution was British: commerce, induced invention, and the scientific revolution”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x), *Economic History Review* 64 (2011), 357–384; [author's explanation of the argument](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/why-was-industrial-revolution-british).

Supports a specific high-wage, cheap-energy interpretation. It is an interpretation to compare with competing accounts, not a settled single-cause explanation. Chapter 6 adds Humphries's critique, engineering sources, and the Song comparison under S36–S38. Detailed regional and colonial comparisons remain worthwhile for a longer revision.

## S05

**Stationary principles.** Richard Feynman, Robert Leighton, and Matthew Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, [I.26, optics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_26.html) and [II.19, action](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_19.html).

Supports the distinction between a variational characterization of physical motion and an intentional search for a goal. Stationarity is not a theorem that history takes the fastest route to maximum energy extraction.

## S06

**Physical asymmetries.** ATLAS Collaboration, [“Probing fundamental symmetries of nature with the Higgs boson”](https://atlas.cern/Updates/Briefing/CP-Symmetry-Higgs); CERN, [future Higgs research](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/higgs-boson/future/).

Orientation to symmetry questions and open research. Distinguish parity violation, CP violation, electroweak symmetry breaking, and the unexplained cosmic matter excess. These sources do not establish assembled constants or dimensions.

## S07

**Thermodynamic proposals.** L. M. Martyushev and V. D. Seleznev, [“The restrictions of the Maximum Entropy Production Principle”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2068) (2013 preprint). Roderick C. Dewar and Amos Maritan, [“The second law, maximum entropy production and Liouville's theorem”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1088) (2011 preprint).

Starting points for the assumptions and disputed scope of maximum-entropy-production arguments. Neither licenses treating all far-from-equilibrium systems as universal maximizers. Any use of a minimum-production theorem must state its near-equilibrium and boundary assumptions.

## S08

**Energy-rate density.** Eric J. Chaisson, [“Energy Rate Density as a Complexity Metric”](https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~ejchaisson/reprints/EnergyRateDensity_I_FINAL_2011.pdf) (2011).

Research lead for comparing energy flow per unit mass across systems. A proposed comparative metric is not proof that energy throughput causes complexity or that evolution maximizes the metric. Full analysis and comparison of system boundaries remain to be done.

## S09

**Commodity relations.** Karl Marx, *Capital*, volume I, [chapter 1, especially section 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch01.htm).

Primary text for commodity fetishism. Chapters 9 and 11 also consult [chapter 6 on labor-power](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch06.htm), [chapter 23 on simple reproduction](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch23.htm), and the *Grundrisse* passage under S53. The book's phrase about relationships between people appearing as relationships between things is a paraphrase, not a newly verified quotation. The proposed energy-and-assembly synthesis belongs to this manuscript.

## S10

**Social systems.** Niklas Luhmann, *Social Systems*, English translation by John Bednarz Jr. with Dirk Baecker (Stanford University Press, 1995), [digitized text](https://uberty.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Niklas_Luhmann_Social_Systems.pdf).

Relevant full-text passages on communication and action were consulted for chapter 9, especially printed pages 137–142; system/environment and interpenetration sections supply the boundary distinctions. No direct quotation is used. Luhmann's communication-centered account should not be silently equated with Marx's class analysis or biological autopoiesis. The original Varela–Maturana–Uribe paper and Ashby's text are now linked under S33 and S45. The larger *Autopoiesis and Cognition* volume remains a further reading lead, not a book reviewed in full here.

## S11

**Money and banks.** Michael McLeay, Amar Radia, and Ryland Thomas, Bank of England, [“Money creation in the modern economy”](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy) (2014).

Supports distinguishing commercial-bank deposit creation from the claim that all money is issued directly by governments. It does not settle the historical origin of money or prove that taxation is money's only source of demand.

## S12

**Cumulative culture.** Michael Muthukrishna and Joseph Henrich, [“Innovation in the collective brain”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780534/), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* (2016).

Supports investigating how social learning, connectivity, and accumulated knowledge affect innovation. Population size is not a complete causal explanation. A detailed cassava-processing scene is not used; chapters 5 and 11 retain the general transmission mechanism without inventing ethnographic details.

## S13

**Early Homo sapiens.** Jean-Jacques Hublin et al., [“New fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco and the pan-African origin of Homo sapiens”](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22336), *Nature* (2017).

Supports the deep history of our species, roughly 300,000 years. It does not support biological stasis, an idle early humanity, or one uniquely dated departure from Africa. Obtain archaeological and genetic work on multiple dispersals and admixture before drafting those details.

## S14

**Cooperation beyond humans.** Alicia P. Melis, Brian Hare, and Michael Tomasello, [“Chimpanzees Recruit the Best Collaborators”](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AAAS/Melis_Chimpanzees_Science_2006_1555100.pdf), *Science* 311 (2006), 1297–1300, DOI 10.1126/science.1123007.

Experimental evidence against the categorical assertion that chimpanzees cannot cooperate. It leaves open important differences in the scale and organization of human cooperation.

## S15

**Domestication hypothesis.** Adam S. Wilkins, Richard W. Wrangham, and W. Tecumseh Fitch, [“The ‘Domestication Syndrome’ in Mammals: A Unified Explanation Based on Neural Crest Cell Behavior and Genetics”](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/197/3/795/5935921), *Genetics* 197 (2014), 795–808.

This is a proposed developmental explanation, not proof of a general human-domestication mechanism. It supplies no basis for racial rankings of intelligence, equating compliance with intelligence, or interpreting modern IQ trends as rapid genetic change. Chapter 11 pairs the proposal with the Lord et al. critique under S51 and does not assert a demonstrated human mechanism.

## S16

**Y-chromosome bottleneck.** Tian Chen Zeng, Alan J. Aw, and Marcus W. Feldman, [“Cultural hitchhiking and competition between patrilineal kin groups explain the post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04375-6), *Nature Communications* (2018). Compare [“Patrilineal segmentary systems provide a peaceful explanation for the post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-47618-5) (2024).

Competing models make this a useful causal-inference case. Neither establishes that money caused the bottleneck. A genetic effective-population estimate is not a direct census of men alive.

## S17

**Industrial nitrogen.** Nobel Foundation, [chemistry prize history](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/themes/the-nobel-prize-in-chemistry-the-development-of-modern-chemistry/); BASF, [Carl Bosch and industrial ammonia](https://www.basf.com/global/en/who-we-are/history/Carl-Bosch).

Supports Haber, Bosch, and ammonia production; BASF's [1913 chronology](https://www.basf.com/global/en/who-we-are/history/chronology/1902-1924/1913) dates industrial operation at Oppau. Supplement the institutional account with independent industrial, labor, agricultural, military, and environmental histories. Do not invent a numerical estimate of lives sustained by fertilizer.

## S18

**Health and productive capacity.** A. Michael Lincoff et al., [“Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes”](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563), *New England Journal of Medicine* (2023).

The trial concerns a defined population with established cardiovascular disease. It does not establish that a drug can substitute for universal health provision, that everyone should receive it, or that preventive interventions always save money. Use only to frame a research question about production, prevention, access, and financing.

## S19

**Stellar context.** NASA Science, [stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/).

Orientation to stellar life cycles. Chapter 4 adds primary nucleosynthesis evidence under S55 and oxygenation evidence under S34; its opening remains a concise material history rather than a complete account of planet formation. Do not describe the Sun as demonstrably among the first stars able to support life.

## S20

**Quantum uncertainty and interpretation.** H. P. Robertson, [“The Uncertainty Principle”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.34.163) (1929); David Bohm, [“A Suggested Interpretation of the Quantum Theory in Terms of ‘Hidden’ Variables. I”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.85.166) (1952).

Chapter 2 distinguishes a constraint on measurement-outcome distributions from an assertion that irreducible chance is the uniquely established quantum ontology. This is not a complete treatment of interpretations or Bell-type constraints.

## S21

**The quantum vacuum.** David Tong, [Quantum Field Theory, section 2, “Free Fields”](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S2.html), especially section 2.3.

Supports the elementary field-theory description of a vacuum as a structured lowest-energy state. Chapter 2 explicitly limits the setting; the argument distinguishing that state from philosophical nothingness is the book's own.

## S22

**Symmetry breaking and the Higgs evidence.** David Tong, [Statistical Field Theory, “From Spins to Fields”](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/sft/sfthtml/S1.html); Peter W. Higgs, [“Broken Symmetries and the Masses of Gauge Bosons”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.13.508) (1964); [ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.7214) and [CMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.7235) discovery papers (2012).

Supports the limited magnetic example and the experimental Higgs context. Gauge redundancy is not identified with physically different magnet orientations. The chapter does not claim that all mass has one origin or provide a complete history of the mechanism's contributors.

## S23

**Parity, CP, and baryogenesis.** C. S. Wu et al., [“Experimental Test of Parity Conservation in Beta Decay”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.105.1413) (1957); J. H. Used to separate three questions. The review supplies conceptual background, not a current experimental-status survey or a claim that cosmic matter asymmetry has been explained.

## S24

**Statistical entropy and initial conditions.** Feynman, Leighton, and Sands, [I.46, “Ratchet and Pawl”](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_46.html); Joel L. Lebowitz, [“Macroscopic laws, microscopic dynamics, time's arrow and Boltzmann's entropy”](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~lebowitz/PUBLIST/lebowitz_360.pdf), *Physica A* 194 (1993), 1–27.

Supports the statistical explanation and the need to account for low-entropy initial conditions. The discussion does not assert exact time-reversal symmetry of all fundamental interactions or derive the origin of time itself.

## S25

**Effective theories.** Joseph Polchinski, [“Effective Field Theory and the Fermi Surface”](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9210046), TASI 1992 lectures, revised 1999; [HTML text](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9210046).

Supports descriptions organized by energy scale. Chapter 2 distinguishes the scale dependence of a description from historical changes in measured constants. The proposed extension to assembled fundamental laws is not a result of this source.

## S26

**The vacuum-energy problem.** Sean M. ## S27

**Evidence of cosmic acceleration.** Adam G. Riess et al., [“Observational Evidence from Supernovae for an Accelerating Universe and a Cosmological Constant”](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9805201) (1998); Saul Perlmutter et al., [“Measurements of Omega and Lambda from 42 High-Redshift Supernovae”](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9812133) (1999).

Supports the historical account of the supernova evidence. Establishing acceleration is distinguished from establishing the ultimate nature of dark energy.

## S28

**Observer selection.** Steven Weinberg, [“Anthropic Bound on the Cosmological Constant”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.59.2607), *Physical Review Letters* 59 (1987), 2607–2610.

Supports the specific bound concerning gravitationally bound structures. The chapter's discussion of probability distributions and linked parameters is a methodological argument, not a claim that this paper demonstrates an ensemble of universes.

## S29

**A dynamical proposal.** Peter W. Graham, David E. Kaplan, and Surjeet Rajendran, [“Cosmological Relaxation of the Electroweak Scale”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07551), *Physical Review Letters* 115 (2015), 221801; [full text consulted](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.07551).

Chapter 2 uses the evolving field and stopping barriers as a concrete example of how a developmental hypothesis can be specified. The proposal is not treated as experimentally established or as a solution to the cosmological constant problem. Its additional physics and cosmological assumptions remain part of its explanatory cost.

## S31

**AI and productive energy capabilities.** International Energy Agency, [“AI for energy optimisation and innovation”](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/ai-for-energy-optimisation-and-innovation), in Energy and AI (2025).

## S32

**Assembly-theory criticism.** Abicumaran Uthamacumaran et al., [“On the salient limitations of the methods of assembly theory and their classification of molecular biosignatures”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41540-024-00403-y), *npj Systems Biology and Applications* 10, 82 (2024).

## S33

## S34

**Early atmospheric chemistry and oxygenation.** James Farquhar, Huiming Bao, and Mark Thiemens, [“Atmospheric Influence of Earth's Earliest Sulfur Cycle”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10926533/), *Science* (2000). ## S35

## S45

## Outstanding research and attribution

- **Unverified attributions:** the supplied [YouTube reference](https://youtu.be/D5in5EdjhD0) and Russell/Newton/Darwin/Harari fragments remain unused as quotations. No dialogue, speaker attribution, or interpretation has been reconstructed from the inaccessible video.
- **Longer historical revision:** deepen case-level evidence with Chinese accounts of the Opium War, industrial labor records, agricultural variation, and specific enclosure and cotton histories. The first draft uses bounded documented examples and explicitly illustrative scenarios. A stirrup-caused-feudalism story, a monetary cause of the Y-chromosome bottleneck, and unverified Norman-Conquest links are omitted.
- **Theory and cosmology:** compare the synthesis with rival explanations, independent assembly-theory validation, and alternative physical models. Chapters 2 and 12 do not supply a quantitative solution to fine-tuning or derive physical constants from social or biological analogies.
- **Economics and welfare:** chapter 10 chooses U.S. historical crises and separates monetary regimes. A policy edition would need jurisdiction-specific evidence and distributional estimates before recommending housing, debt, health, or ecological programs.
- **Cognition and AI:** the chapter uses bounded language, representation, self-modeling, and control studies. Broader cognitive claims and industrial feedback require further experiments; benchmark gains, consciousness, deployment, and net productive benefits remain separate questions.
- **Publication review:** the complete first draft is not a comprehensive literature review. Extend scenes and comparisons where they add explanatory value, verify any new quantitative claims, and consolidate the repeated conceptual qualifications without removing substantive limits.
