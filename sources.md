# Working source ledger

Sources checked during drafting and structural revision on November 14–15, 2026. This is a research foundation for first drafts, not a completed bibliography. Search excerpts and abstracts support only the narrow claims identified below; relevant full-text passages were also consulted for the cosmological chapter, including variational principles, the vacuum, effective theories, initial conditions, and the relaxion mechanism. Comprehensive literature review and scene-level verification remain necessary where specified. Source keys are used in `ideas`, `outline`, and chapter notes; the prologue and all twelve prose chapters have numbered notes. The afterword sets the causal-inference standard and identifies its proposed research designs. Abstracts and indexed passages are identified as limited evidence; access restrictions are not treated as full-text review.

## S01

**Constraint satisfaction.** Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, [constraint-satisfaction chapter](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/4th-ed/pdfs/newchap05.pdf); UC Berkeley CS188, [variable ordering](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/textbook/csp/ordering.html).

Supports the minimum-remaining-values heuristic and early detection of impossible branches. It does not establish a universal algorithm used by nature. Chapter 1's question about the creative role of bottlenecks is the book's extension from this limited example; the computational sources do not establish a general physical mechanism of creation. The opening Sudoku experience comes from the author's notes; no benchmark results or reconstructed dialogue are claimed.

## S02

**Assembly theory.** Abhishek Sharma et al., [“Assembly theory explains and quantifies selection and evolution”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06600-9), *Nature* 622 (2023), 321–328.

Starting point for assembly index, copy number, and the authors' account of selection. The index depends on construction rules and reusable components; it is not a direct transcript of actual history. Claims of universal applicability and biosignature specificity require independent scrutiny. Chapter 3 now presents a bounded account alongside the independent criticism in S32; broader validation and the controversy remain open.

## S03

**Metabolic innovation and historical contingency.** Zachary D. Blount, Christina Z. Borland, and Richard E. Lenski, [“Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of Escherichia coli”](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0803151105), *PNAS* (2008).

Supports the example of aerobic citrate use in the long-term evolution experiment. Related mechanistic research: Erik M. Quandt et al., [“Recursive genomewide recombination and sequencing reveals a key refinement step…”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3926077/), *PNAS* (2014). Distinguish the initial innovation, its historical preconditions, and later refinement.

## S04

**Steam and economic constraints.** Robert C. Allen, [“Why the industrial revolution was British: commerce, induced invention, and the scientific revolution”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x), *Economic History Review* 64 (2011), 357–384; [author's explanation of the argument](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/why-was-industrial-revolution-british).

Supports a specific high-wage, cheap-energy interpretation. It is an interpretation to compare with competing accounts, not a settled single-cause explanation. Chapter 5 retains Humphries's critique and engineering sources under S36–S37; the longer Song and imperial comparisons under S37–S38 remain in the archived steam draft. Detailed regional and colonial comparisons remain worthwhile for a longer revision.

## S05

**Stationary principles.** Richard Feynman, Robert Leighton, and Matthew Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, [I.26, optics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_26.html) and [II.19, action](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_19.html).

Supports the distinction between a variational characterization of physical motion and an intentional search for a goal. Stationarity is not a theorem that history takes the fastest route to maximum energy extraction.

The action discussion was rechecked for I66 and chapter 8's combined fine-tuning hypothesis, including its explicit distinction between stationarity and a minimum. The source supports the component principle; it does not derive cosmic parameter values or the universe's necessity.

## S06

**Physical asymmetries.** ATLAS Collaboration, [“Probing fundamental symmetries of nature with the Higgs boson”](https://atlas.cern/Updates/Briefing/CP-Symmetry-Higgs); CERN, [future Higgs research](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/higgs-boson/future/).

Orientation to symmetry questions and open research. Distinguish parity violation, CP violation, electroweak symmetry breaking, and the unexplained cosmic matter excess. These sources do not establish assembled constants or dimensions.

## S07

**Thermodynamic proposals.** L. M. Martyushev and V. D. Seleznev, [“The restrictions of the Maximum Entropy Production Principle”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2068) (2013 preprint). Roderick C. Dewar and Amos Maritan, [“The second law, maximum entropy production and Liouville's theorem”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1088) (2011 preprint).

Relevant full-text passages were read: Dewar–Maritan's stationary-state argument under forcing constraints and its heuristic status, and Martyushev–Seleznev's local force–flux formulation and restrictions. Chapter 2 uses them to motivate and delimit the broader assembly conjecture, not to claim an established universal law of discovery or evolution. Minimum-production results likewise require their particular assumptions.

## S08

**Energy-rate density.** Eric J. Chaisson, [“Energy Rate Density as a Complexity Metric”](https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~ejchaisson/reprints/EnergyRateDensity_I_FINAL_2011.pdf) (2011).

The paper compares energy flow per unit mass across systems. Section 5 was read for its emphasis on optimal ranges and its explicit refusal to infer a universal power- or entropy-production extremum from the data. It supports an energy–complexity research connection, not an independent proof of the manuscript's maximizing hypothesis. Wider evaluation of the metric and system boundaries remains open.

## S09

**Commodity relations.** Karl Marx, *Capital*, volume I, [chapter 1, especially section 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch01.htm).

Primary text for commodity fetishism. Chapter 11 also draws on [chapter 6 on labor-power](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch06.htm) and [chapter 23 on simple reproduction](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch23.htm); chapter 10 uses the *Grundrisse* passage under S53. The book's phrase about relationships between people appearing as relationships between things is a paraphrase, not a newly verified quotation. The proposed energy-and-assembly synthesis belongs to this manuscript.

## S10

**Social systems.** Niklas Luhmann, *Social Systems*, English translation by John Bednarz Jr. with Dirk Baecker (Stanford University Press, 1995), [digitized text](https://uberty.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Niklas_Luhmann_Social_Systems.pdf).

Relevant full-text passages on communication and action were consulted for chapter 11, especially printed pages 137–142; system/environment and interpenetration sections supply the boundary distinctions. No direct quotation is used. Luhmann's communication-centered account should not be silently equated with Marx's class analysis or biological autopoiesis. The original Varela–Maturana–Uribe paper and Ashby's text are now linked under S33 and S45. The larger *Autopoiesis and Cognition* volume remains a further reading lead, not a book reviewed in full here.

## S11

**Money and banks.** Michael McLeay, Amar Radia, and Ryland Thomas, Bank of England, [“Money creation in the modern economy”](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy) (2014).

Supports distinguishing commercial-bank deposit creation from the claim that all money is issued directly by governments. It does not settle the historical origin of money or prove that taxation is money's only source of demand.

## S12

**Cumulative culture.** Michael Muthukrishna and Joseph Henrich, [“Innovation in the collective brain”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780534/), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* (2016).

Supports investigating how social learning, connectivity, and accumulated knowledge affect innovation. Population size is not a complete causal explanation. A detailed cassava-processing scene is not used; chapters 5 and 10 retain the general transmission mechanism without inventing ethnographic details.

## S13

**Early Homo sapiens.** Jean-Jacques Hublin et al., [“New fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco and the pan-African origin of Homo sapiens”](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22336), *Nature* (2017).

Supports the deep history of our species, roughly 300,000 years. It does not support biological stasis, an idle early humanity, or one uniquely dated departure from Africa. Chapter 9 adds a bounded archaeological comparison under S72 and a climatic model under S73. Detailed dispersal and admixture histories still need fuller treatment.

## S14

**Cooperation beyond humans.** Alicia P. Melis, Brian Hare, and Michael Tomasello, [“Chimpanzees Recruit the Best Collaborators”](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AAAS/Melis_Chimpanzees_Science_2006_1555100.pdf), *Science* 311 (2006), 1297–1300, DOI 10.1126/science.1123007.

Experimental evidence against the categorical assertion that chimpanzees cannot cooperate. It leaves open important differences in the scale and organization of human cooperation.

## S15

**Domestication hypothesis.** Adam S. Wilkins, Richard W. Wrangham, and W. Tecumseh Fitch, [“The ‘Domestication Syndrome’ in Mammals: A Unified Explanation Based on Neural Crest Cell Behavior and Genetics”](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/197/3/795/5935921), *Genetics* 197 (2014), 795–808.

This is a proposed developmental explanation, not proof of a general human-domestication mechanism. It supplies no basis for racial rankings of intelligence, equating compliance with intelligence, or interpreting modern IQ trends as rapid genetic change. Chapter 9 and the parked development essay pair the proposal with the Lord et al. critique under S51; neither asserts a demonstrated human neural-crest mechanism.

## S16

**Y-chromosome bottleneck.** Tian Chen Zeng, Alan J. Aw, and Marcus W. Feldman, [“Cultural hitchhiking and competition between patrilineal kin groups explain the post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04375-6), *Nature Communications* (2018). Competing models make this a useful causal-inference case. Neither establishes that money caused the bottleneck. A genetic effective-population estimate is not a direct census of men alive.

## S17

## S18

**Health and productive capacity.** A. Michael Lincoff et al., [“Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes”](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563), *New England Journal of Medicine* (2023).

The trial concerns a defined population with established cardiovascular disease. It does not establish that a drug can substitute for universal health provision, that everyone should receive it, or that preventive interventions always save money. Use only to frame a research question about production, prevention, access, and financing.

## S19

Orientation to stellar life cycles. ## S20

**Quantum uncertainty and interpretation.** H. P. Robertson, [“The Uncertainty Principle”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.34.163) (1929); David Bohm, [“A Suggested Interpretation of the Quantum Theory in Terms of ‘Hidden’ Variables. I”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.85.166) (1952).

Chapter 6 distinguishes a constraint on measurement-outcome distributions from an assertion that irreducible chance is the uniquely established quantum ontology. This is not a complete treatment of interpretations or Bell-type constraints.

Bohm's original abstract was rechecked for I68's stronger ontological commitment. Its deterministic account supports the distinction between quantum predictions and a unique ontology. The manuscript's claims that nothingness is impossible and constitutive indeterminacy entails natural emergence and continual becoming are philosophical proposals, not results attributed to Robertson or Bohm.

## S21

Supports the elementary field-theory description of a vacuum as a structured lowest-energy state. Chapter 6 explicitly limits the setting; the argument distinguishing that state from philosophical nothingness is the book's own.

## S22

Tong's equilibrium-magnetization discussion was rechecked for I67. It supports separating an ordered or asymmetric state from a claim of thermodynamic disequilibrium. The extension to the cosmic origins of disequilibrium remains the book's hypothesis.
