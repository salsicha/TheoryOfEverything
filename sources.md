# Working source ledger

Sources checked during preparation on November 14, 2026. This is a research foundation for a first draft, not a completed bibliography. Search excerpts and abstracts support only the narrow claims identified below. Full-text review, page references, competing scholarship, and scene-level verification remain necessary where specified. Source keys are used in `ideas` and the chapter outlines.

## S01

**Constraint satisfaction.** Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*, [constraint-satisfaction chapter](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/4th-ed/pdfs/newchap05.pdf); UC Berkeley CS188, [variable ordering](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/textbook/csp/ordering.html).

Supports the minimum-remaining-values heuristic and early detection of impossible branches. It does not establish a universal algorithm used by nature. The opening Sudoku experience comes from the author's notes; no benchmark results or reconstructed dialogue are claimed.

## S02

**Assembly theory.** Abhishek Sharma et al., [“Assembly theory explains and quantifies selection and evolution”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06600-9), *Nature* 622 (2023), 321–328.

Starting point for assembly index, copy number, and the authors' account of selection. The index depends on construction rules and reusable components; it is not a direct transcript of actual history. Claims of universal applicability and biosignature specificity require independent scrutiny. Read methods, responses, and critical literature before drafting chapter 3 in full.

## S03

**Metabolic innovation and historical contingency.** Zachary D. Blount, Christina Z. Borland, and Richard E. Lenski, [“Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of Escherichia coli”](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0803151105), *PNAS* (2008).

Supports the example of aerobic citrate use in the long-term evolution experiment. Related mechanistic research: Erik M. Quandt et al., [“Recursive genomewide recombination and sequencing reveals a key refinement step…”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3926077/), *PNAS* (2014). Distinguish the initial innovation, its historical preconditions, and later refinement.

## S04

**Steam and economic constraints.** Robert C. Allen, [“Why the industrial revolution was British: commerce, induced invention, and the scientific revolution”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x), *Economic History Review* 64 (2011), 357–384; [author's explanation of the argument](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/why-was-industrial-revolution-british).

Supports a specific high-wage, cheap-energy interpretation. It is an interpretation to compare with competing accounts, not a settled single-cause explanation. Research regional wages, women's labor, coal transport, colonial trade, and Chinese industrial history before expanding the scene.

## S05

**Stationary principles.** Richard Feynman, Robert Leighton, and Matthew Sands, *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, [I.26, optics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_26.html) and [II.19, action](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_19.html).

Supports the distinction between a variational characterization of physical motion and an intentional search for a goal. Stationarity is not a theorem that history takes the fastest route to maximum energy extraction.

## S06

**Physical asymmetries.** ATLAS Collaboration, [“Probing fundamental symmetries of nature with the Higgs boson”](https://atlas.cern/Updates/Briefing/CP-Symmetry-Higgs); CERN, [future Higgs research](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/higgs-boson/future/).

Orientation to symmetry questions and open research. Distinguish parity violation, CP violation, electroweak symmetry breaking, and the unexplained cosmic matter excess. These sources do not establish assembled constants or dimensions.

## S07

**Thermodynamic proposals.** L. M. Martyushev and V. D. Seleznev, [“The restrictions of the Maximum Entropy Production Principle”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2068) (2013 preprint). Roderick C. Dewar and Amos Maritan, [“The second law, maximum entropy production and Liouville's theorem”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1088) (2011 preprint).

Starting points for the assumptions and disputed scope of maximum-entropy-production arguments. Neither licenses treating all far-from-equilibrium systems as universal maximizers. Any use of a minimum-production theorem must state its near-equilibrium and boundary assumptions.

## S08

**Energy-rate density.** Eric J. Chaisson, [“Energy Rate Density as a Complexity Metric”](https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~ejchaisson/reprints/EnergyRateDensity_I_FINAL_2011.pdf) (2011).

Research lead for comparing energy flow per unit mass across systems. A proposed comparative metric is not proof that energy throughput causes complexity or that evolution maximizes the metric. Full analysis and comparison of system boundaries remain to be done.

## S09

**Commodity relations.** Karl Marx, *Capital*, volume I, [chapter 1, especially section 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch01.htm).

Primary text for commodity fetishism. The book's phrase about relationships between people appearing as relationships between things is a paraphrase, not a newly verified quotation. The proposed energy-and-assembly synthesis belongs to this manuscript.

## S10

**Social systems.** Niklas Luhmann, *Social Systems*, English translation by John Bednarz Jr. with Dirk Baecker (Stanford University Press, 1995), [digitized text](https://uberty.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Niklas_Luhmann_Social_Systems.pdf).

Located for full-text study, especially communication and action. Verify passages before quotation. Luhmann's communication-centered account should not be silently equated with Marx's class analysis or biological autopoiesis. Also obtain Maturana and Varela's *Autopoiesis and Cognition* and Ashby's *An Introduction to Cybernetics* for the conceptual comparison; these are reading leads, not sources already checked in full.

## S11

**Money and banks.** Michael McLeay, Amar Radia, and Ryland Thomas, Bank of England, [“Money creation in the modern economy”](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy) (2014).

Supports distinguishing commercial-bank deposit creation from the claim that all money is issued directly by governments. It does not settle the historical origin of money or prove that taxation is money's only source of demand.

## S12

**Cumulative culture.** Michael Muthukrishna and Joseph Henrich, [“Innovation in the collective brain”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780534/), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* (2016).

Supports investigating how social learning, connectivity, and accumulated knowledge affect innovation. Population size is not a complete causal explanation. Check the cited cassava literature and local ethnographic context before using a detailed food-processing scene.

## S13

**Early Homo sapiens.** Jean-Jacques Hublin et al., [“New fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco and the pan-African origin of Homo sapiens”](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22336), *Nature* (2017).

Supports the deep history of our species, roughly 300,000 years. It does not support biological stasis, an idle early humanity, or one uniquely dated departure from Africa. Obtain archaeological and genetic work on multiple dispersals and admixture before drafting those details.

## S14

**Cooperation beyond humans.** Alicia P. Melis, Brian Hare, and Michael Tomasello, [“Chimpanzees Recruit the Best Collaborators”](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AAAS/Melis_Chimpanzees_Science_2006_1555100.pdf), *Science* 311 (2006), 1297–1300, DOI 10.1126/science.1123007.

Experimental evidence against the categorical assertion that chimpanzees cannot cooperate. It leaves open important differences in the scale and organization of human cooperation.

## S15

**Domestication hypothesis.** Adam S. Wilkins, Richard W. Wrangham, and W. Tecumseh Fitch, [“The ‘Domestication Syndrome’ in Mammals: A Unified Explanation Based on Neural Crest Cell Behavior and Genetics”](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/197/3/795/5935921), *Genetics* 197 (2014), 795–808.

This is a proposed developmental explanation, not proof of a general human-domestication mechanism. It supplies no basis for racial rankings of intelligence, equating compliance with intelligence, or interpreting modern IQ trends as rapid genetic change. Seek critiques and independent human evidence before further use.

## S16

**Y-chromosome bottleneck.** Tian Chen Zeng, Alan J. Aw, and Marcus W. Feldman, [“Cultural hitchhiking and competition between patrilineal kin groups explain the post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-04375-6), *Nature Communications* (2018). Compare [“Patrilineal segmentary systems provide a peaceful explanation for the post-Neolithic Y-chromosome bottleneck”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-47618-5) (2024).

Competing models make this a useful causal-inference case. Neither establishes that money caused the bottleneck. A genetic effective-population estimate is not a direct census of men alive.

## S17

**Industrial nitrogen.** Nobel Foundation, [chemistry prize history](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/themes/the-nobel-prize-in-chemistry-the-development-of-modern-chemistry/); BASF, [Carl Bosch and industrial ammonia](https://www.basf.com/global/en/who-we-are/history/Carl-Bosch).

Starting points for Haber, Bosch, and ammonia production. Supplement the institutional account with independent industrial, labor, agricultural, military, and environmental histories. Do not invent a numerical estimate of lives sustained by fertilizer.

## S18

**Health and productive capacity.** A. Michael Lincoff et al., [“Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes”](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563), *New England Journal of Medicine* (2023).

The trial concerns a defined population with established cardiovascular disease. It does not establish that a drug can substitute for universal health provision, that everyone should receive it, or that preventive interventions always save money. Use only to frame a research question about production, prevention, access, and financing.

## S19

**Stellar context.** NASA Science, [stars](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/).

Orientation to stellar life cycles. Before a full chapter draft, add primary work on nucleosynthesis, element dispersal, neutron-capture processes, planetary formation, and oxygenation. Do not describe the Sun as demonstrably among the first stars able to support life.
